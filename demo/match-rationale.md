# Match rationale — why this job scored the way it did for Priya

> ILLUSTRATIVE DEMO ARTIFACT — hand-authored for this example to show how Jobbee *explains* a match. The scores below are illustrative, not a live product run. Inputs: `demo/jd-fintech-api-pm.md` (the job) × `examples/priya-anand/` (her workspace files).

**Match: 82 / 100** — strong match, one flagged trade-off, zero clarifications needed.

---

## What raised the score — from her files, quoted

**TN-1 eligibility ↔ sponsorship offered.** The JD says *"We sponsor TN-1 and H-1B."* Her `Applicant Profile.md` states under Visa / Work Authorization: *"US work authorization: TN-1 eligible (Canadian citizen; employer support letter required, renewable in 3-year increments, no lottery/cap)."* A job that sponsors is a different job for a TN-eligible candidate — this fact pair alone is why the Phase-0 gate never paused on her file.

**API platform depth, both directions.** The JD asks for *"a shipped API platform decision… versioning policy, pricing model, deprecation program, or build-vs-buy call."* Her files carry **all four**:

- versioning policy + deprecation → `Projects/Developer Api Platform.md` and `Projects/Api Sunset Legacy V1.md` (retired v1 with 4,000 dependent customers, zero incidents — the JD's "3,000+ integrated partners" deprecation fear is a thing she's already done at larger scale);
- pricing model → the usage-based redesign, including the +34% result *and* the walked-back launch with its 18% churn ("bring the one that still stings a little; we'll ask what you'd change" — her files have that answer pre-written);
- build-vs-buy → the tokenization-core decision with its honest vendor-friction ledger.

**The developer-facing clarification answer, cited.** The JD says "developer-facing platforms." Ambiguity risk: some companies say "platform" and mean internal infrastructure. Her `## Clarifications` log already answers this: *"Developer-facing, specifically API products… 'Platform' in my files always means something a developer integrates against."* Jobbee matched the JD's usage to her recorded definition — no pause required, because she'd answered the question before it existed.

**Culture fit is stated on both sides, verbatim-adjacent.** JD: *"strategy docs here get argued with, not nodded at"* / *"worked with engineers who push back."* Her file: *"A written-strategy culture. Docs that get argued with"* / *"Engineers who push back. The best product calls I've made lost the first argument."* When a candidate's stated values mirror the JD's stated values this closely, the interview-signal confidence is high.

**The nice-to-haves she was born with.** JD nice-to-haves read like her file's table of contents: started as an engineer (4 years at Ferroline), marketplace/payments economics (Cartway seller payments), 0→1 internal platform to company standard (`Projects/Zero To One Internal Tooling.md`), US+UK market experience (the UK payments launch, FCA registration and all).

## What pulled the score down — and why it's a trade-off, not a disqualifier

**Compensation sits below her US band floor.** Her file: *"$340K–$420K total comp… Base matters to me."* The JD: $330–380K **base** + equity. Read carefully, base-only vs. her TC band isn't automatically a miss — with equity the package likely clears $340K+ TC. But her floor is stated as TC-ish and the JD's equity is unquantified, so Jobbee flags it rather than assuming: **match on role, open question on package** — the kind of thing a first conversation resolves in five minutes, and exactly what the flag is for.

**The London interest goes unused — this time.** Her ranked location list has UK/London at #3 with the Global Talent route named, and the JD mentions *"we're opening a London entity next year."* That's a *future* option, not this role's location (NYC hybrid), so it doesn't raise the score — but Jobbee notes it as relationship context: a company planting a UK flag is more interesting to her than its NYC-only competitor, even at equal scores.

**"140 people, profitable" — untested against her preferences.** Her want-list (CEO access at Principal, design partnership) and won't-list (feature factory, sales-led roadmap) are answerable only in process; the JD *signals* the right answers ("work directly with the CEO," "docs get argued with") but signals aren't evidence. Score impact: neutral, watch-item listed.

## The clarifications her file pre-answered

This match needed **zero pauses** from Jobbee. Two places where a thinner file would have stopped the search:

1. **Citizenship / work auth** — absent, Jobbee's Phase-0 gate fires the visa question and the match waits on email. Priya answered it long ago; the answer built her `## Visa / Work Authorization` section, and the TN-1 bullet did the matching work above.
2. **"Platform" ambiguity** — a file that says "platform product experience" without saying *which kind* invites the wrong short-list. Her clarification log pins the definition (developer-facing API products), so the JD matched against meaning, not against a keyword.

## The lesson this file is here to teach

Rich files → fewer pauses → better matches. Every fact Priya wrote down — citizenship, the meaning of "platform," her comp floor, her London interest, her culture wants — converted a point where Jobbee would have had to *ask* (or *guess*) into a point where Jobbee could *match*. The two clarifications in her history weren't friction; they were the system converting missing facts into permanent ones. This is why the workspace files reward completeness: Jobbee can only score what you've told it, and it pauses on exactly what you haven't.
