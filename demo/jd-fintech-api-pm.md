# Principal Product Manager, API Platform

**Thornfield Pay** · New York, NY (hybrid) · Full-time

> ILLUSTRATIVE DEMO ARTIFACT — this is a fictional job posting written for the Jobbee examples repo. Thornfield Pay does not exist; any resemblance to a real company is coincidental.

## About Thornfield Pay

Thornfield Pay builds payment infrastructure for mid-market platforms: embedded payments, payout orchestration, and the developer platform that ties them together. 140 people, profitable, ~1,900 platform customers, growing. The developer platform team owns the public API surface, SDKs, docs, and the partner experience — the part of the product our customers touch first and judge hardest.

## The role

We're hiring a Principal Product Manager to own the API platform's next phase: from "well-liked API with an enterprise motion" to a platform mid-market developers choose on their own. You'll report to the VP Product and work directly with the CEO on platform strategy — Principal here means you own a strategy the org argues with, not a roadmap you administer.

## What you'll do

- Own the API platform strategy: the multi-year direction for the public API surface, versioning and deprecation policy, SDK and docs experience, and the partner onboarding funnel (median time-to-first-API-call is a metric this team lives and dies by).
- Redesign usage-based pricing and packaging for the API as adoption scales past the enterprise-only motion — you'll own the model, the defense of it to finance, and the migration plan for existing partners.
- Lead a major version transition without breaking trust: our current API has 3,000+ integrated partners, several of them settlement-critical, and the deprecation program you run will be a trust product as much as a technical one.
- Work with the regulatory team on money-transmission licensing implications for new API capabilities in two new markets.
- Recruit and develop 2–3 product managers; set the writing bar — strategy docs here get argued with, not nodded at.
- Be the escalation path for top-20 partner integrations; you should want to be in the integration Slack channel at least sometimes.

## What we're looking for

- 8+ years of product management, with significant time on **developer-facing platforms or payments products** (both, ideally).
- A shipped API platform decision you can defend at depth: a versioning policy, a pricing model, a deprecation program, or a build-vs-buy call — bring the one that still stings a little; we'll ask what you'd change.
- Experience killing or migrating something legacy without torching customer trust.
- Writing that gets argued with: you can produce a 6-page strategy doc that survives a hostile review meeting and comes out better.
- Comfort operating with regulatory constraints (payments, licensing, or adjacent).
- Engineer collaboration in the specific sense: you've worked with engineers who push back, and your specs got better for it.

## Nice to have

- You started as an engineer and still write small tools — you read API docs for fun and it shows.
- Marketplace or payments economics: take rates, payout timing, risk holds.
- Experience across 0→1 (internal platform to company standard) and scale (pricing changes, partner migrations).
- Cross-border product experience: US and UK/EU markets, or UK/EU market entry specifically.

## Compensation & logistics

- **$330–380K base** + equity (meaningful, late-stage private company, secondary program twice a year).
- Hybrid: 3 days/week in our NYC office (SoHo); remote considered for exceptional candidates in US time zones.
- **We sponsor TN-1 and H-1B** — visa cases are routine for us, not exceptional; say so in your application and we'll handle it like it's normal, because here it is.
- US or UK coverage experience valued; we're opening a London entity next year.

## Our hiring process

Two steps: a working session with the VP Product and CEO (90 minutes — you'll review a real platform decision of yours and we'll argue with it politely), then a half-day with the team (engineering leads + a partner-facing session + a pricing case). No take-home decks; we read your docs, we don't collect them.
