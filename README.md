# Jobbee Workspace Examples

Example Jobbee workspaces — real-looking, entirely fictional people, showing exactly what to put in the three files that do the work:

- **Applicant Profile** (`Applicant Profile.md`) — how Jobbee reads your situation when it searches for jobs
- **Base Resume** (`Base Resume.md`) — your resume's bigger sibling: everything that didn't fit on the page
- **Project files** (`Projects/*.md`) — at least one file per project, capture each and every detail you know. A project is any substantial piece of work, in your job or outside it ([what counts](#what-counts-as-a-project)). Length follows the work: a few weeks of work can fit in half a page, while a multi-year program can need an overview plus companion files that share its name (see [`maya-chen` · Ledger Core Rewrite](examples/maya-chen/Projects/Ledger%20Core%20Rewrite.md))

> [Jobbee](https://jobbee.io) finds your jobs and writes your resumes. These example workspaces show the input side of that: the workspace files you own, written the way Jobbee reads them.

## What's here

Each folder under `examples/` is one complete example workspace. Pick the persona closest to your situation — profession *and* the use-case column. The roles span business, legal, healthcare, design and people work as well as engineering: fields where hiring runs through resume screens, people apply to many jobs, and the pay makes the hours spent tailoring each application worth saving.

## What counts as a project

A project is any substantial piece of work: at your job, at school, or on your own time. It can be a whole initiative or one situation you handled. If it took real work and ended up as one bullet on your resume, or didn't make it onto your resume at all, it deserves its own project file. `dana-whitfield` shows both: her Ledgerline campaign is one bullet on her resume, her creator partnership isn't there at all, and each has a full file. `maya-chen`'s payouts reconciliation is the engineering version: a nine-week feature that became one line.

## Clarifications

There's no schema to satisfy: plain notes work. When a file is **ambiguous or missing a fact that matters**, Jobbee asks you a clarification question by email and saves your answer into `Applicant Profile.md`, so the same question doesn't come up twice. See it in [`diego-ramirez`](examples/diego-ramirez/Applicant%20Profile.md) (he was asked about his visa, and his answer became his visa section) and [`priya-anand`](examples/priya-anand/Applicant%20Profile.md) (two questions, both answered). Contrast on purpose: [`maya-chen`](examples/maya-chen/Applicant%20Profile.md) states her citizenship in her file, so she was never asked about it — her one question was about comp vs remote.

## Using an example

1. Pick the persona closest to your level.
2. Read `Applicant Profile.md` first — it's the file Jobbee reads when searching for your jobs.
3. Open `Base Resume.md` — the longer, fuller record your tailored resumes are cut from.
4. Skim `Projects/` — depth is the point. The bar for "enough": the file should hold the answer to any relevant interview question about that project.

Then write yours. Plain, honest notes beat polished prose — Jobbee reads for facts, not grammar.

## Repository layout

Files appear here with the same names you see in the Jobbee editor (the workspace file tree), not the internal storage paths.

```
examples/<persona>/
  Applicant Profile.md    # Applicant Profile — read when searching for your jobs
  Base Resume.md          # Base Resume — what your tailored resumes are cut from
  Projects/*.md           # Project files — at least one per project, no limit
```
