# Job Applicant — Paul Brennan

Facts about my search that the settings can't hold. I've built for the web since 2004, so this file also says which of those years matter for the job I want, and which don't.

## What I'm looking for

- Staff Frontend Engineer, individual contributor, focused on design systems, accessibility and web performance. Principal is fine where a company's ladder puts this work there. I'm a Senior Frontend Engineer at Shelfwright today, and I've led cross-team frontend work there since 2021.
- Experience: 22 years building for the web, since 2004. The years that matter for this role start in 2012, when I moved from agencies to product companies, and most of my evidence is from 2019 on. The 2004–2012 agency years are summarized in my resume on purpose. They taught me things I still use, but they aren't the reason to hire me.
- Location: Kansas City, MO. Remote within the US is my preference. Hybrid in Kansas City is fine, up to 3 days a week. I'm not relocating. Travel up to about 4 trips a year.
- Compensation: current base $184K, a 10% target bonus, and private-company options I value at zero for planning. Looking for $215K–$245K base and $280K–$350K total comp. My base floor is $210K where the equity is liquid (public-company RSUs) and $225K at a private company, for the same reason I value my current options at zero.
- Notice: currently employed. Three weeks.
- Why now: my work has been staff-shaped for a while, and Shelfwright's web org had one Staff opening in 2025. It went to a colleague with a strong case, and there is none in 2026. I'd rather find the title where the work already is than wait for a slot.

## Where I have real depth

- Design systems. I led the rebuild that replaced three component libraries at Shelfwright with one token-based React system: 9 of 11 product teams on it, and a merchant theming model that went from about 900 lines of override CSS to 38 tokens. I know the hard part is adoption, not components.
- Accessibility. I led the remediation of 318 WCAG 2.1 AA audit findings to meet the European Accessibility Act date, including 3 serious issues in a component I had built myself. I test with NVDA, JAWS, VoiceOver and TalkBack, not only with automated checks, because automation could detect only a third of what the audit found.
- Web performance, measured in the field. Checkout's mobile p75 LCP went from 4.1s to 2.3s and INP from 410ms to 170ms, and a holdout measured the effect on completed checkouts, with error bars I quote.
- Incremental migrations. AngularJS to React over 31 months with no feature freeze, codemods across about 1,900 files for the design system, and a Flash to HTML5 rebuild in 2011. I plan a migration so the product keeps shipping, and I report risk retired, not files changed.
- Platform shifts. I've worked through table layouts to CSS, Flash to HTML5, jQuery to single-page apps, and AngularJS to React. What lasted every time was semantic HTML and content kept separate from the code that renders it.

## Search constraints — please respect these

- Individual contributor only. No engineering manager roles and no player-coach roles with direct reports. I led a team of 3 developers at an agency from 2010 to 2012, including their reviews. I was fine at it, and it isn't the work I want.
- The Staff scope has to be real for frontend. If a company's staff path stops at senior for frontend engineers, or the role is a senior role with a new title, it isn't a fit.
- Frontend-heavy: at least about 70% of the role in the browser. My backend is Node for server rendering and backend-for-frontend layers, and I won't pretend to more for a full-stack role.
- No ad-tech, and no businesses built on third-party tracking. Third-party tags were the biggest single cost in the checkout work I led. I won't build the product that adds them.
- No agencies or consultancies. I did eight years of client work. Design systems and accessibility programs need a product to live in for years.
- At least about 5 product teams shipping UI. A design system or an accessibility program needs teams to serve.

## How I work

- I write the RFC before the code, and the decision doc always prices the "do nothing" option.
- I measure with field data and real devices. My default test phone is a mid-range Android, not my own phone.
- Every component I own gets tested with a keyboard and at least two screen readers before it ships.
- Migrations are incremental: codemods, a fixture set built from real files, and a rollback for every step.
- I mentor in the open: a weekly frontend office hour since 2022, and code review that explains the why.
- I keep my project notes current. The files in `Projects/` are how I think, not something I wrote for a search.
