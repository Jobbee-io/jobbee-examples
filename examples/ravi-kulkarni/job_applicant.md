**Q:** You're on an H-1B. Are you open to transferring it to a new employer, and how does that interact with your green card process?
**A:** Yes, open to transfer — with clear eyes about what it costs. Mechanics first: an H-1B transfer to a new employer uses premium processing, so I can start about 2–3 weeks after the petition goes in; there's no lottery and no waiting-out-a-clock like the cap-subject process. The real cost is the green card, not the visa. My PERM (ETA-9089) is filed with my current employer, but my I-140 is NOT yet filed — their policy files after PERM approval. Priority dates come from PERM filing only if the I-140 later gets approved and is ported; if I change employers *before* any I-140 is filed, my green card process at the new employer starts from zero: new PWD, new PERM, new wait. AC21 portability — the provision that lets you keep your green card process when switching after 180 days — only applies once an I-140 is approved and an I-485 adjustment is pending, and I'm not there. So the trade-off I'm actually weighing in any move: X months at a better job vs. restarting a 2+ year PERM pipeline at the new employer, and pushing my priority date back by however long that takes. I'm not saying that to scare anyone off — sponsorship-capable employers do this, and if the I-140 gets filed at my current employer before I leave, the restart question changes shape. I just want it discussed in the first conversation, like the salary.

**Q:** (Follow-up from the same session) Given that, would you actually leave?
**A:** Honestly: for the right experimentation role, yes — that's why we're having the conversation rather than me sitting quietly. The role and the team decide it, priced against a known, quantifiable green card cost. I'd rather make that trade deliberately than resent a job I took passively.
