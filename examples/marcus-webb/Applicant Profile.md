# Job Applicant — Marcus Webb

Facts about my search. I run a tight filter list because in banking, fit *is* the product — the wrong platform wastes your best years, and I've watched good bankers rot on the wrong desk. Better to state the filters than to discover them in month four.

## What I'm looking for

- **Director or VP-level investment banker**, Technology, Media & Telecom (TMT) or fintech coverage — M&A and capital raising, execution-inclusive. The eventual arc is MD with sponsorship; I'm not applying for it, I'm making myself impossible not to promote.
- 15 years in: analyst → associate → VP → Director. I've staffed, run, and defended processes end to end; the next seat needs real deal responsibility, not a bigger title over the same responsibilities.
- **Location:** New York is home base. Open to Chicago or San Francisco for the right platform — "right" defined below, and the bar is high enough that I mean it.
- **Industries with real depth:** TMT M&A (software particularly — vertical SaaS, infrastructure software, the whole consolidation wave), fintech capital raising (payments, B2B infrastructure, the private fintech market's post-2022 repricing).

## Compensation — the structure matters more than the number

- Current: $450K all-in ($225K base + bonus).
- **Floor: $225K base.** This is the filter that saves everyone time: any offer structured mostly-bonus with a light base is a hard no, immediately, regardless of the headline. Here's why it's rational, not proud: base is the only part of banker comp that doesn't depend on a bonus pool decision made about a year you no longer control. I've watched two cycles destroy people who took "high OTE, low base" seats in good markets. The number protects the downside; the deal flow protects the upside.
- Headline upside matters, but only layered on that base: $300K+ all-in realistic, with MD-track economics visible within a defined window (I'll ask what the last three promotions took, in years and in dollars — every good shop can answer).

## How I filter — explicit, with reasons

These are real filters, not preferences. Jobbee should treat them as hard unless noted:

1. **VPs still model.** I will only join shops where VPs and Directors still build and own models — where seniority means better judgment on the model, not escape from the model. Reason: bankers who stop modeling stop being promotable anywhere else, and the shops that run VPs as staffing managers produce MDs with no product. This is the single biggest predictor of whether a seat grows you.
2. **No pure-coverage roles without execution.** A coverage seat that never touches execution produces bankers who can talk but can't close. I need live processes in my quarters — ideally a mix of sell-side M&A, buyside, and financing.
3. **Sector exclusions on values:** no firearms manufacturers or dealers as clients, no predatory lending (payday, title-loan at scale). Not squeamishness — I've done hostile and ugly-adjacent work fine — these two are lines, and they're permanent.
4. **Deal-team size 6–10.** Under 6 means there's no analyst leverage and the VP is doing analyst work (I've done it; it's a feature only in your twenties). Over 10 means the work is fractionated until nobody owns anything. I ask the deal-team composition question early and I read hesitation as data.
5. **Two-step interview processes only.** I'll do a superday; I won't do a five-round rolling process across two months. Reason: a shop that can't decide on its own hires in two meetings runs its deal processes the same way, and that indiscipline lands on juniors.
6. **Staffing honesty.** If staffing is "the MD you know gets the staffing," I want that said plainly in the process rather than discovered. Every shop claims meritocratic staffing; I calibrate by asking juniors, not HR.
7. **Bonus deferral structure disclosed up front.** Multi-year deferrals are normal and fine — undisclosed ones are a values signal, and I've seen the clause appear at offer stage exactly once. Never again.
8. *(Soft filter, not a hard line)* MD promotion path with a real internal example in the last three years. If the last two MD promotions were lateral hires, the internal path is decorative and I'd price that in rather than refuse the seat.
