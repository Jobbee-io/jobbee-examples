## The deal that died — and what it taught

Project Harbinger (2019, infrastructure software, ~$400M ask). I ran the process for a founder-controlled client. Four bidders in, two into diligence, and the data told us clearly that the bids would clear below the founder's walk-away. The process died in the third quarter — correctly.

What I learned, in the order I learned it:

1. **Telling a founder "don't sell" is the hardest sell there is,** and the only one where the banker's commission says one thing and the advice says another. I said it anyway, with the numbers, in a room with his CFO (a skeptic, which helped).
2. **Process design is advice.** The two-round structure I built gave him four credible data points instead of one negotiated auction — that's why he believed the read. A one-round auction would have produced a price he'd have spent years believing was "left on the table."
3. **A dead deal done honestly is a franchise.** He sent us Meristem (deal #6) two years later, and the Quillon introduction came through his CFO's network. The revenue from "the deal we told him not to do" exceeds what the killed deal would have paid.

I keep this one first in the story when clients ask why they should trust me on process design. The answer is that I've priced my own fee against a client's interest and can show the receipt.
