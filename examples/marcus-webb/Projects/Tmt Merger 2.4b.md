# TMT merger — Corrigan Software / Aldervane Systems ($2.4B)

Marbury Cross, 2018–2019, sell side. I was the VP who led the financial-analysis workstream on a $2.4B software merger, through a second request and a retention problem. This is the overview: the deal, the timeline, the workstream map, what I owned, and the retention work in full. Two angles are big enough for their own files. Companion files: `Tmt Merger 2.4b Valuation And Fairness.md` (the merger model, the synergy build and its defense, the fairness analyses, the collar decision) and `Tmt Merger 2.4b Regulatory And Closing.md` (the second request, the outside date, the financing, funds flow and the working-capital peg).

## The deal

- **Corrigan Software** (our client): vertical SaaS for residential property managers. About $290M of 2018 revenue, about 85% recurring, growing 14%, adjusted EBITDA about $64M, about 2,400 customers. Private. Founder-CEO; a PE sponsor held 40% and was in year 5 of its hold.
- **Aldervane Systems** (the buyer): listed, software for commercial property and facilities. About $210M of revenue growing 31%, about a 25% adjusted EBITDA margin, about $4.1B of market cap before announcement.
- **Consideration:** $1.0B in cash and $1.4B in Aldervane stock at a fixed exchange ratio. Announced value $2.4B, about 8.3x Corrigan's 2018 revenue. Corrigan's holders ended up with about 25% of the combined company.
- **Overlap:** two product lines competed head to head, resident payments and maintenance work orders. Everything else was complementary.

## Process and timeline

- April 2018: Corrigan's board hires Marbury Cross. A targeted process: 7 parties contacted (5 strategic, 2 sponsors), 4 NDAs signed.
- July 2018: two first-round bids. Aldervane at $2.2B in cash and stock; a sponsor at $2.15B in cash.
- October 2018: final round. Aldervane goes to $2.4B. The sponsor's bid stayed live until signing: a stalking horse we never had to use.
- November 2018: signed and announced. Marbury Cross delivered its fairness opinion to Corrigan's board at signing.
- Late December 2018: second request, about 6 weeks after signing.
- Late April 2019: substantial compliance certified, about 40,000 documents produced.
- May 2019: the original outside date passes and the automatic 3-month extension kicks in. Later that month the waiting period expires with no action and no divestiture conditions.
- June 2019: closed.

## The retention work

The synergy model committed to $95M of annual run-rate synergies. About $47M of it (the R&D consolidation of the two overlapping product lines, and cross-selling) ran through teams led by about 40 named people. With Corrigan's head of HR, I identified the 40, priced a retention pool of $14M (cash plus Aldervane RSUs, half vesting 12 months after close and half at 24 months), and built the sequence for who would be told what, and when.

The uncomfortable finding: two of the 40 were executives outside the confidentiality wall. Offering them retention meant telling them about the merger. We told them last, 48 hours before announcement, with counsel in the room.

Three moments from it:

1. **A diligence question that was really a leak.** In second-stage diligence, Aldervane's integration lead asked a question that could only be answered by naming one of the 40. Counsel caught it in the Q&A review. I drafted the answer at role level, with no name. In a merger a diligence answer is a document, and every document is a retention risk.
2. **The founder's ask.** The founder wanted to tell his key people early. A decent instinct, but it would have put 40 confidentiality walls at risk before announcement. I built him an alternative: a retention framework the board approved in advance but didn't execute, so he could make a credible promise without revealing names or terms. He took it. Nobody else needed to know that a spreadsheet had saved the announcement.
3. **The one who left anyway.** 37 of the 40 signed before announcement. Two of the three who didn't left before close; both roles had named successors in the plan. The third was the head of the overlapping work-orders product line. He held his unsigned offer through close and resigned six weeks after. His reason was his new reporting line, to Aldervane's head of product, not money. We had priced his package right and his manager's support wrong. Every retention analysis I've run since has a "manager of the retained" column.

The framework (the role-level analysis, the advance-approved terms, the told-last list) became the template on two later deals I ran.

## What I actually owned

The merger model and every number in the board decks. The bottom-up synergy build, and its defense in two negotiation sessions where Aldervane's bankers tried to cut $20M of synergies to argue the exchange ratio down. The economic-support package for the second request. The retention analysis and framework. Funds flow and the peg. My associate ran the data room; my two analysts were the best two I've worked with; the MDs sold the deal and negotiated price. That division of labor is the honest shape of a VP seat at its best.

## Numbers I can defend

- $2.4B announced value: $1.0B cash, $1.4B stock, about 8.3x 2018 revenue.
- $95M of run-rate synergies, bottom-up by function. The function split is in the valuation companion, and I can rebuild it from memory.
- About 40,000 documents produced in the second request; zero divestiture conditions.
- Retention: 37 of 40 signed at announcement, a $14M pool, the framework reused on two later deals.
- One outside-date extension. The peg dispute settled at $8.2M against a $6–12M modeled range.

## What I'd do differently

- Start the market-definition work at signing, not when the second request arrived. The overlap was obvious, and we spent the first three weeks after the request building data that had sat in both companies' CRMs all along.
- Ask for price protection on the stock before price was agreed. Aldervane's shares were down 11% at close, so the stock portion was worth about $150M less than announced. I modeled a collar; we asked for it after price was set, when we had the least leverage. Detail in the valuation companion.
- Put the reporting line in the retention model from day one, not after the departure that taught me to.

## What's still open

Nothing is open: the deal closed in June 2019 with no divestiture conditions, and the working-capital peg settled at $8.2M on the final weekend. What came of the synergies two years later is in the valuation companion.
