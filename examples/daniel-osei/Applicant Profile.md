## How I write about client work

Almost everything I've done belongs to a client. New York's confidentiality rule is broader than privilege: it also covers information from the work that could embarrass or harm the client, or that the client asked me to keep confidential. So every client matter in my files is written the same way:

- No client, counterparty or deal code names, even for deals that were announced. A deal being public doesn't make the diligence findings public.
- Deal size as a band ($500M–$1B), never the exact figure.
- Industry one level up ("developer-security software", not the product), and company size as a band.
- Dates to the month or quarter.
- My role, the type of issue, the decision, and the kind of term that solved it ("a special indemnity backed by a separate escrow"). Not the advice itself, and no negotiated number unless it's rounded into a band or a percentage.
- Counts and cycle times about my own work come from my own matter log, rounded.
- The test before anything goes in: could the client or the other side recognize their matter from this paragraph? If yes, I take details out until they couldn't.

In an interview I'll answer follow-ups at the same level of detail. If a question needs more than that, I'll say that's where I stop. An in-house team should want a lawyer who stops there.

## How I work

- Answer first. A business question gets a recommendation in the first sentence, then the risk in plain terms (how likely, how big, what it costs to fix later), then the detail for whoever wants it.
- The business owns the decision; I own how clear the risk is. I learned that on secondment, partly by getting it wrong.
- If I answer the same question 3 times, it goes into a playbook with a fallback and an approver.

- I keep a matter log: what I negotiated, what we gave up, and why. The numbers in these files come from it.
