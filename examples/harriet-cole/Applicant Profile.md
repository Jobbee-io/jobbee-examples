Facts about my search that the settings fields can't hold. I've spent 7 years in strategy consulting and I'm leaving it for a strategy role inside a company. My project files are written as engagements with the clients anonymized. Each one says what I did, what the team did, and what the client actually put into practice.

Individual contributor: the senior strategy person who does the analysis and then stays with it until it's running. Titles with "Manager" in them are fine when the role has no direct reports.
- This is a step up and a change of side. Today I'm a Senior Consultant at Corriemoor Partners, a London strategy consulting firm, and I've led 5 engagements day to day since March 2025 (2 of them short due diligences). Consultants at my level often move in at Strategy Manager. I'm aiming for the senior IC level above that.
- About 7 years, all at Corriemoor: Business Analyst (2019–2021), Consultant (2021–2023), Senior Consultant (since September 2023). Mostly retail and consumer clients, plus fintech and software.
- Location: London. Hybrid in London up to 3 days a week in the office, or remote within the UK. I'm not relocating.
- Compensation today: £84,000 base, a discretionary bonus of up to 20% of base (FY2025 paid 14%, £11,760), and a 6% employer pension contribution. No equity.
- Target: £95,000–£115,000 base, and £105,000–£135,000 total cash with bonus. My base floor is £90,000. When I compare offers, I count public-company RSUs at grant value spread over the vesting period, and private-company options at zero.

## Where I have real depth

- Cost programmes in retail. I owned the store labour workstream of a grocery cost programme: a task-based labour model for about 420 stores, and the savings tracker the client's finance team signed off. £21m realised run-rate savings against £28m approved (`Projects/Grocery Cost Transformation.md`).
- Market entry and go/no-go decisions. I built the business case that told a UK payments fintech not to enter Germany, including a paid test that put a real number on acquisition cost (`Projects/Fintech Market Entry No Go.md`).
- Integration programme offices. I ran the integration office for a £310m software acquisition for 10 months: Day 1 readiness, the synergy tracker and its definitions, and the handover to the client's own team (`Projects/Integration Programme Office.md`).
- Operating model design. Decision rights first, then structure, then spans and layers, for a 410-role commercial function at an online fashion retailer (`Projects/Commercial Operating Model.md`).
- Commercial due diligence for private equity clients: 13 of them, 2 to 4 weeks each, mostly retail and consumer.
- Tools: Excel at an advanced level (operating and business-case models, not LBO or valuation models), PowerPoint, Alteryx, SQL for joining client data extracts, Tableau for simple dashboards.

## What I don't have yet

- My outcomes happened inside client companies, and client staff made each change real. My Base Resume defines how I count outcomes so the line between my part and theirs is clear.
- I haven't worked day to day with product teams. The closest is a pricing engagement for a software company, which is also my clearest failure (`Projects/Pricing Hypothesis Postmortem.md`).

- No consulting firms, Big Four advisory, or interim and contract roles. The point of this move is to own what happens after the recommendation.

- Tech, fintech or retail (grocery, general merchandise, e-commerce). No betting and gaming, and no tobacco or vaping. Those are personal lines.
- Public companies, or private ones with roughly £100m+ revenue or at Series C and later. Earlier than that, "strategy" is usually a generalist role working straight for the CEO. That's a good job, just not the one I'm after.
- London hybrid at 3 office days a week or fewer, or remote within the UK. Travel up to 20%. I've spent most weeks of the last 7 years on client sites 3 or 4 days a week, and I don't want that again.
- Base floor £90,000.
- Former clients: my contract stops me joining a company I worked for in my last 12 months at Corriemoor, for 6 months after I leave, unless the firm agrees. I check this myself (see Clarifications).

## How I work

- Answer first. The first line of anything I write is the recommendation, then the 3 facts it rests on.
- Every hypothesis gets a kill test: the result that would prove it wrong, written down in week 1 and run first. I started this after the pricing engagement I got wrong.
- Definitions before numbers. I agree how a number is counted with its finance owner before the first figure goes out, and I keep identified, approved, implemented and realised apart.
- I stay past the deck. 5 of the 6 engagements in my project files ran into implementation or had a measurement follow-up I asked for.
- Client confidentiality. My files name industries and sizes, not clients. In an interview I'll go deeper on the work, not on who it was for.
