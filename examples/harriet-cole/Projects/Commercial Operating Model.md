# Operating model redesign for an online fashion retailer's commercial function

Corriemoor Partners, for a UK online fashion retailer. September 2025 – February 2026, plus a 90-day review in June 2026, as the day-to-day engagement lead. On my resume this is one bullet with 4 numbers. This file holds the decisions, the people consequences, how each number is counted, and 2 mistakes that cost the client 3 weeks and some trust.

## What it was, and why

The client sells own-brand and third-party fashion online, about £850m revenue. Its commercial function (buying, merchandising, trading, planning and pricing) had 410 roles. Gross margin fell 2.1 points in FY2024/25, and late markdowns were a big part of it. A markdown needed a weekly committee and 2 sign-offs: a median of 9 days from the sell-through report to a new price on the site. There were 7 layers between the Chief Commercial Officer and the most junior roles, and a new range took 30 weeks from brief to launch.

The CCO asked for a new operating model: who decides what, and a structure to match.

## My role

I led the engagement day to day with 2 consultants and an analyst. The partner spent about a day a week, mostly with the CCO. I owned the design principles, the decision-rights charter, the spans and layers analysis and the implementation plan. The consultants built the role catalogue and ran the process mapping. The CCO owned the final design, and the HR director owned selection and consultation. The people decisions were the client's.

## How it went

We interviewed 46 people and mapped 63 recurring commercial decisions. 24 went into the decision charter, each with one owner, the inputs the owner must get, and guardrails. For example, a category trading lead can mark down up to 30% within the category's markdown budget without sign-off; anything deeper goes to the head of category.

The structure followed: 9 category teams with buying, merchandising and trading together in each, and planning and pricing as shared teams.

Phase 2 (December – February) was implementation support: role mapping, the new decision forums, and a dashboard of the measures below. At my request, the statement of work included a 90-day review, which I ran in June 2026.

## The numbers, and their definitions

- Layers, from the CCO to the most junior commercial role: 7 → 5.
- Average span of control, meaning direct reports per people manager, filled roles only: 3.6 → 5.9.
- Roles in the commercial function: 410 → 384. Of the 26 fewer, 15 were vacancies not backfilled and 11 were redundancies after individual consultation. 9 more people whose roles changed moved into new roles.
- Markdown decision time: median days from the weekly sell-through report to the price change going live, from the pricing system's change log. 9 days (March – May 2025) → 2 days (March – May 2026).
- Range critical path, brief to launch: 30 weeks before. The first range run fully on the new model (autumn/winter 2026) took 27 weeks against 26 planned (client figure, September 2026).
- I don't claim gross margin. The client reports it up 0.8 points in the first half of 2026, but weather and a supplier change are in that number too. That's why this is only one bullet on my resume.

## What went wrong

I started with boxes. In weeks 2 to 4 I showed the CCO 3 structure options before the decision work was finished, because the CCO wanted to see options early. The CCO picked option B in week 4. In weeks 5 to 7 the decision charter showed that option B gave markdown decisions to a role that didn't hold the stock data. We redesigned, and it cost 3 of the 10 design weeks. Decisions first, structure second: I knew that, and I let the client's impatience set the order.

A draft got out through my process. A workshop deck in week 6 included a draft structure with role titles and no names. It went to 40 people, and they could work out which roles weren't on it. The HR director heard about it from her own team. I apologised to her directly that day. From then on, drafts lived in a restricted folder with a version log, and nothing structural went to a wide group before HR signed it off.

I also planned 2 weeks for individual consultation. It took 5, which pushed go-live from January to February.

## What I'd do differently

- Hold structure options back until the main decisions are mapped, and explain why on day 1.
- Agree the document rules with HR before the first workshop.
- Plan consultation on HR's timeline, not mine.
