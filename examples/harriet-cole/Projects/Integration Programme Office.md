# Integration management office for a £310m software acquisition

Corriemoor Partners, for a UK payroll software company. September 2023 – July 2024, 10 months, as a Senior Consultant. On my resume this is one bullet. This file covers what the integration office did, what I owned, how the synergy numbers are defined, the payroll mistake I signed off, and the handover.

## What it was

The client (about £180m revenue, about 1,400 people) bought a smaller UK competitor (about £55m revenue, about 420 people, about 9,000 payroll customers) for £310m. Completion, Day 1, was 6 November 2023. The client had planned to run the integration in-house and brought us in 6 weeks before Day 1, when it became clear nobody had the time.

The deal model promised £21m of run-rate synergies by month 24: £16m of cost (property, duplicate software tools and hosting, overlapping finance and HR roles) and £5m of revenue (selling the client's HR module to the acquired company's payroll customers).

## My role

I ran the integration management office day to day for the client's integration director, a client employee. In months 1 to 4 I had 2 consultants and part of an engagement manager's time; in months 5 to 10, 1 consultant. The partner came to one steering committee a month.

Mine: the operating rhythm (weekly workstream reviews, a fortnightly steering committee), the Day 1 readiness checklist, the synergy tracker and its definitions, the risk and issue log, and the handover to the client's permanent team. Not mine: the content of the 14 workstreams (finance, HR, payroll, product and engineering, sales, customer migration, IT, property, legal and others), each led by a client leader; the synergy targets, which came from the deal model; and every decision about people.

## Definitions, agreed with the CFO in week 2

- Baseline: the combined FY2023 budgeted cost base of both companies before the deal.
- Run-rate cost synergy: the annualised reduction against the baseline, counted only when the action is finished and the cost has left the ledger (the contract has ended, the person has left), signed off by a finance business partner.
- One-off costs to achieve: tracked separately and never netted against synergies.
- Revenue synergy: annual recurring revenue from one company's customers buying the other's product, counted at contract signature, reported on its own line and never added to the cost number in the headline.

## How it went

Day 1 had a 312-item checklist across the 14 workstreams. 309 items were green on the day and 3 were amber with agreed workarounds. Day 1 itself was quiet, which is the goal.

The first synergy problem came in month 3. Workstream leads were reporting their own progress, and the tracker said £9.4m of run-rate cost synergies. When the finance business partners checked against the definition, £5.1m qualified. The rest was roles "agreed to exit" but still on the payroll, and software contracts with notice given that ran until 2025. I added a status ladder (identified, planned, actioned, realised) and made finance sign-off the only way to reach realised. 2 workstream leads pushed back hard for a month. The steering committee backed the definition, helped by the fact that the CFO had signed it in week 2.

Revenue was the second problem. The £5m assumed 15% of the acquired payroll customers would buy the HR module in year 1. The client's own history, selling HR to its own payroll customers, was about 6% a year. I showed this in month 4. The steering committee kept the target but agreed to report revenue on its own line, so cost overdelivery couldn't hide a revenue gap.

## The numbers

- At my handover, end of July 2024, about month 9 after Day 1: £11.2m realised run-rate cost synergies against the £16m target. By type: £4.7m roles, £3.9m software and hosting, £2.6m property.
- Revenue synergy at handover: £0.9m of ARR against £5m.
- One-off costs at handover: £8.7m against a £10m budget.
- Month 18 (May 2025), from a client board summary shared with Corriemoor: £15.1m cost and £1.8m revenue. I haven't checked those against the ledger.

## What went wrong

The payroll migration. In January 2024 the acquired company's 420 employees moved onto the client's payroll system. The checklist item was "payroll parallel run passed", and I marked it green on a parallel run of 50 test records, not the full population. The bank details for 37 people failed validation, and the exception report went to a mailbox nobody owned. Those 37 were paid 2 days late, by a payroll software company. They got a same-day transfer and an apology from the CEO. The payroll workstream owned the process, but I signed the checklist, so the miss is mine. After that, every checklist item needed its evidence written next to it: what was tested, on how many records, and by whom.

The handover. The client hired a permanent integration manager in May 2024 and we overlapped for 8 weeks. After I left, steering went monthly and customer migration onto the client's platform slipped by 2 quarters. That isn't mine to own, but next time I'd hand the meetings over earlier and support the client's manager from the room, instead of running them myself until my last week.

## What I'd do differently

- Agree what "green" means for each checklist item, with its evidence, before anyone marks one.
- Hand the operating rhythm to the client's own manager halfway through the overlap, not at the end.
- Put revenue synergies on their own line from week 1, not from month 4.

## What's still open

When I handed over at the end of July 2024, £4.8m of the £16m cost target was still to be realised, revenue synergy was £0.9m against £5m, and customer migration onto the client's platform was still ahead. The month-18 summary, the last figures I have, showed cost at £15.1m and revenue at £1.8m, with 6 months left to the month-24 target. I don't know how either target ended, or where the migration stands now.
