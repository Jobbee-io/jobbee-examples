# Turnaround territory — bottom decile to 120% at Fennec

Fennec Systems, 2021–2022. I joined Fennec in April 2021 into a carved-together territory that had produced **$1.1M closed the prior year against a $3.4M peer average** — bottom decile of an eight-AE org. In 2022 that same patch closed **$6.5M, 120% of a $5.4M plan**, and I was promoted to Principal AE on the result. This file is the honest version: what I actually changed, what the numbers show, and the luck I'm not going to pretend wasn't there.

## What I inherited

- **A patch, not a territory.** Six states stitched together from three departed AEs' books. No account history in CRM past 90 days, no notes, two logins dead on arrival.
- **A repo of zombie pipeline.** 63 "open" opportunities, $8.9M weighted. My first-month audit found: 41 were dead (contact gone, product discontinued, or already bought something else), 12 were real but undersized, 10 were live. The previous AE's attainment wasn't a work-ethic story; it was a hygiene story wearing a work-ethic costume.
- **A product with a reputation problem.** Fennec's deployment automation was liked by every engineer who used it and distrusted by every VP who'd seen a deployment tool fail in public. Nobody in the patch had run a structured pilot in two years.

## What I changed

**1. Coverage plan from zero, by hand.** I rebuilt the territory as a named-account plan in three weeks: 400 accounts triaged on (a) deployment-chaos signals — job posts mentioning terraform churn, outage postmortems, cloud-migration press; (b) reachable buying committee — could I name two stakeholders in 48 hours; (c) no incumbent conflict. The triage cut 400 accounts to a working patch of 70 named targets, tiered 15/25/30. Coverage, not activity, was the product.

**2. Disqualification discipline.** This is the unpopular half of the turnaround and the half that made it work. I killed **41 of 63** open opportunities in month one — with written reasons, sent to my manager *before* I closed them, so the decision was a record and not a vibe. Undersized deals got a minimum-size gate ($120K TCV) instead of open eligibility. Two reps on the team thought I was insane to erase 40% of my open pipeline in week three. Both watched my close rate double by Q4.

**3. The pilot-as-first-motion fix.** Every new-logo deal ran the same structured pilot template: 60–90 days, three success criteria the *customer writes*, a go/no-go memo the customer authors. It's the structure I built later at Harvestlane (`projects/the-1.5m-three-year-deal.md`); at Fennec scale it was five of these running at once, each ~$40–60K, each converting at 80%+ because the customer owned the evidence.

**4. The multi-threading floor.** No deal advanced past "qualified" with a single stakeholder. Non-negotiable, and it's the reason Junipertree survived its champion's layoff at month 11.

## The attainment curve

| Period | Closed | Against plan | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2020 (predecessor, FY) | $1.1M | vs $3.4M peers | the inheritance |
| 2021 Q2–Q4 (my first 9 mo) | $2.7M | $2.5M plan → 108% | cleanup + 4 pilot conversions |
| 2022 FY | $6.5M | $5.4M plan → **120%** | full-year effect of the rebuild |

The curve isn't a hockey stick; it's a step. Q2 2021 was 94% of segment quota while I audited and killed; the step happens in Q3–Q4 2021 when the first four pilot conversions land and the rebuilt coverage starts producing.

## The luck, disclosed

Two competitors mispriced their 2022 renewals into my territory — one raised list 40% with no usage-justification story, one tried to convert a legacy contract to a consumption model the customer's CFO publicly hated. I closed both displacements (a combined $1.4M). Honest ledger: **roughly 22% of my 2022 number was competitor error, not my craft.** I'd rather name it than let an interviewer find it; the other 78% was the coverage plan, the disqualification discipline, and five pilots run the same way. And on the headline itself, one rounding note in my own disfavor: 120%, not 121% — the plan was $5.4M, the number is $6.5M, and I don't round up.

## What I'd carry to the next territory (and what I wouldn't)

- **Carry:** the week-one audit (kill dead pipeline with written reasons), the 48-hour named-stakeholder test, minimum deal-size gates, customer-authored pilot memos, the 2.5× coverage floor.
- **Not carry:** the pace. I did the audit and the coverage plan while carrying a live quota, and it cost me two weekends a month for a quarter. In a fresh seat I'd ask for one ramp quarter of reduced target to do the same work in daylight. A territory turnaround shouldn't be a heroics story; the whole point of writing this file is that it's a *process* story that happens to have a 120% on it.
- What I'd want from the company side, asked at first call, not month four: patch history, predecessor attainment, and the honest reason the seat is open. I've now been on both sides of that conversation and I know what a real answer sounds like.
