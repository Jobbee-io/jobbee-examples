# Job Applicant — Elena Vargas

Facts about my search. I sell infrastructure for a living, which means I know exactly what an unfocused search costs, so this file is written the way I'd write a territory plan: explicit filters, numbers I can defend, and the reasons attached.

## What I'm looking for

- **Enterprise Account Executive**, quota-carrying, on infrastructure, data, or developer-tools products. 13 years in revenue roles (SDR → mid-market AE → enterprise AE → principal AE, 10 of them carrying an AE number), currently L7-equivalent at a data-platform company. I want the next seat to be bigger territory, bigger deals, same craft — not a move into "strategic accounts" that turns out to mean renewal paperwork.
- **Level:** senior enterprise / principal IC track. I've been offered manager seats twice and turned both down deliberately — I'm better and happier as the person on the deal, and I'd rather be a principal AE at a company that respects that track than a first-line manager managing three people to hit my old numbers.
- **Stage:** growth-stage or public. Pre-product-market-fit startups are the wrong seat for my skill set — I run named-account enterprise motions, not founder-led scrums.

## Compensation — the structure is the filter, not the number

Sales comp has more ways to be wrong than any other offer letter, so here is the full structure I need, not just the headline:

- **OTE floor $450K**, minimum **50/50 base/variable split; I prefer 60/40** and will trade headline OTE for the split. Reason in one line: a 40/60 plan makes the company's forecast problem my personal cash-flow problem, and I've watched that movie from the next desk.
- **Base floor $200K.** Non-negotiable, same logic as the split.
- **Uncapped accelerators beyond 100% of quota.** Capped plans are a hard no — a cap is the company telling you its demand forecast ends at quota, which is exactly when your pipeline is hottest and your leverage is highest.
- **Accelerator schedule at or above 1.5× past 100%, 2× past 125%** on incremental. I'll model any plan you put in front of me; ask for the spreadsheet, I have it.
- **Equity on a real vesting schedule** — standard 4-year with a 1-year cliff, quarterly vesting after. No cliff-games (grants that vest in year 3–4 lumps to fake retention), and refreshers documented before I sign, not promised after.
- Current, for calibration: **$520K OTE ($260K/$260K)**, uncapped 1.5×/2.0× accelerators, quarterly vesting — I'm not running from a bad plan; I'm running toward a better one.

## Territory — what I will and won't inherit

- **Named-enterprise territory with Fortune 500 logos in it.** I sell to committees, and committees need companies that have committees. This is a real constraint, not snobbery: my deal craft (14-month cycles, 9-person buying groups, procurement redlines) has no target in an empty map.
- **Not a "building motion" role.** If the territory is a list of logos nobody has called, that's not a territory, it's a hope. I've built one from bottom-decile and documented it (`Projects/Turnaround Territory.md`) — I proved I can, and I don't need to prove it twice.
- **Existing pipeline preferred, and I want the patch history.** Show me the last 8 quarters of bookings, the logo list, and what happened to the last two AEs in the seat. If the honest answer is "the territory churns people," I want that said at first call, not discovered at month four.
- **Ramp quota: 2 quarters maximum.** I ramp fast because the craft transfers (I've changed product categories twice and hit plan in Q3 both times). A 4-quarter ramp plan on an enterprise seat usually means the pipeline is fiction.

## Deal size and product — the picky part

- **Floor: $250K ACV.** I've carried $500K-ACV deals and closed $1.5M TCV multi-year (`Projects/The 1.5m Three Year Deal.md`). SMB-mix roles are excluded — a blended book means the comp plan taxes enterprise effort to subsidize transactional volume.
- **Product filter, and I mean it:** I will only carry infrastructure, data, or developer-tools products **whose architecture I can whiteboard unsolicited** — storage engine layers, metadata catalogs, deployment orchestration, event pipelines. The demo bar for my deals is a CTO in the room, and a CTO smells a rehearsed demo in four minutes. I don't carry SaaS I can't credibly explain to an engineer.
- Products I've carried, so you can place me: marketing analytics (self-serve + mid-market), infrastructure deployment automation (enterprise), and a columnar lakehouse data platform (current). Adjacent categories I'd carry confidently: CI/deploy tooling, observability, API platforms, streaming. What I won't carry: vertical SaaS (HR, legal ops, restaurant tech) — no offense to those teams; I just can't demo them credibly at my deal size.

## What I won't take — stated plainly

- **Credit-split games.** No team-selling arrangements where the SE's contribution gets split out of my credit after the fact, and no "pooled" variable comp. I write the split memo myself, before the deal — I've done it for years and it kills the dispute before it exists.
- **Forced-rank stack-rank cultures.** Rank-ordering AEs by attainment ignores that territories are dealt, not chosen. I'll be measured against my plan and my patch, not against the person holding a hotter map.
- **More than 40% travel.** I have a seven-year-old at home. This is a plain constraint, not a negotiation — I plan my travel in blocks (kickoffs, QBRs, on-sites) and I'm honest that >40% roles should hire someone else.
