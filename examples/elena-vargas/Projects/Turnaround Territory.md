# Turnaround territory — bottom decile to 120% at Fennec

Fennec Systems, April 2021 to December 2022. I joined Fennec in April 2021 into a carved-together territory that had produced **$1.1M closed the prior year against a $3.4M peer average**, bottom decile of an eight-AE org. In 2022 the same patch closed **$6.5M, 120% of a $5.4M plan**, and I was promoted to Principal AE in January 2023 on the result. This file is what I changed, what the numbers show, and the luck I'm not going to pretend wasn't there.

## What I inherited

- **A patch, not a territory.** Six states stitched together from three departed AEs' books. No account history in the CRM older than 90 days, no notes, two logins dead on arrival.
- **Zombie pipeline.** 63 "open" opportunities, $8.9M weighted. My first-month audit: 41 were dead (contact gone, product discontinued, or they had already bought something else), 12 were real but undersized, 10 were live. The previous AE's number wasn't a work-ethic story. It was a hygiene story wearing a work-ethic costume.
- **A product with a reputation problem.** Every engineer who used Fennec's deployment automation liked it. Every VP who'd seen a deployment tool fail in public distrusted it. Nobody in the patch had run a structured pilot in two years.

## What I changed

**1. A coverage plan from zero, by hand.** In three weeks I rebuilt the territory as a named-account plan. I triaged 400 accounts on three tests: (a) deployment-chaos signals, such as job posts mentioning Terraform churn, public outage postmortems, cloud-migration press; (b) a reachable buying committee: could I name two stakeholders in 48 hours; (c) no incumbent conflict. That cut 400 accounts to 70 named targets, tiered 15 / 25 / 30. Coverage, not activity, was the product.

**2. Disqualification discipline.** The unpopular half of the turnaround, and the half that made it work. In month one I closed out 41 of the 63 open opportunities, about $3.6M of the $8.9M weighted, roughly 40% of my pipeline's value. Each one got a written reason, sent to my manager before I closed it, so the decision was a record, not a vibe. Undersized deals got a minimum-size gate ($120K TCV) instead of open eligibility. Two reps on the team thought I was insane to erase 40% of my pipeline in week three. My win rate on qualified opportunities went from 17% on the inherited pipeline to 36% on the rebuilt one by Q4 2021.

**3. Pilot as the first motion.** Every new-logo deal ran the same structured pilot: 60 to 90 days, three success criteria the customer writes, and a go/no-go memo the customer authors, usually the champion's engineers. At Fennec's deal sizes that meant five pilots running at once, each about $40–60K, converting at 80%+ because the customer owned the evidence. Harvestlane's pilot later was a variant: I handed the memo to the skeptics instead of the champion (`Projects/The 1.5m Three Year Deal.md`).

**4. A multi-threading floor.** No deal advanced past "qualified" with a single stakeholder. That's the reason Junipertree survived its champion's layoff at month 11. Harvestlane later taught me the floor should count decision-makers, not contacts.

## The attainment curve

| Period | Closed | Against plan | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2020 (predecessor, full year) | $1.1M | vs. $3.4M peer average | the inheritance |
| 2021, Q2–Q4 (my first 9 months) | $2.7M | $2.5M plan, 108% | cleanup, plus 4 pilot conversions |
| 2022, full year | $6.5M | $5.4M plan, **120%** | the full-year effect of the rebuild |

It's a step, not a hockey stick. Q2 2021 came in at 94% of segment quota while I audited and killed. The step came in Q3 and Q4 2021, when the first four pilot conversions landed and the rebuilt coverage started producing.

## The luck, disclosed

Two competitors mispriced their 2022 renewals into my territory. One raised list price 40% with no usage story to justify it. The other tried to move a legacy contract to a consumption model the customer's CFO publicly hated. I closed both displacements, $1.4M combined. Roughly 22% of my 2022 number was competitor error, not my craft. I'd rather name it than let an interviewer find it. The other 78% was the coverage plan, the disqualification discipline, and five pilots run the same way.

## What I'd carry to the next territory, and what I wouldn't

- **Carry:** the week-one audit (kill dead pipeline with written reasons), the 48-hour named-stakeholder test, minimum deal-size gates, customer-authored pilot memos, the 2.5× coverage floor.
- **Not carry:** the pace. I did the audit and the coverage plan while carrying a live quota, and it cost me two weekends a month for a quarter. In a fresh seat I'd ask for one ramp quarter at reduced target to do the same work in daylight. A turnaround shouldn't be a heroics story. This one is a process story that happens to end at 120%.
- **What I'd ask the company, at the first call:** patch history, predecessor attainment, and the honest reason the seat is open. I've now been on both sides of that conversation, and I know what a real answer sounds like.
