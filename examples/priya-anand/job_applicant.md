- **Function shape matters more than title.** Principal here means: I own a strategy document the org actually argues with, I have direct CEO access, and my influence is measured in what other teams ship because of what I wrote.

- **Developer platforms.** I took an internal payments API to a public developer platform — 2,300 integrated partners, 41B API calls/month, a usage-based pricing model I designed and defended. I've also killed one: retired a legacy API version with 4,000 dependent customers without torching the trust that made the platform worth having.

Started as a backend engineer (4 years) — I still write small tools and I read API docs for fun, which my engineers find mildly unsettling.

## What I want — stated plainly

- **A written-strategy culture.** Docs that get argued with, not decks that get nodded at. I'll ask in interviews what happened to the last strategy doc that was wrong.
- **Direct CEO access at Principal level.** If Principal PMs need a VP to speak to the CEO, the title is decorative. I've had the access and I know what it's worth.
- **Engineers who push back.** The best product calls I've made lost the first argument. I want teams where the second argument happens with data, not hierarchy.

## What I won't take — also stated plainly

- **Feature-factory PM roles.** If the roadmap is a queue and the PM's job is throughput, I'm the wrong hire.
- **No design partnership.** If PMs don't sit with customers and engineers before specs exist, the specs are fiction.
- **Enterprise sales-led roadmaps.** I've watched roadmaps get rewritten by the ten biggest logos and I won't do it again.
- **Ads targeting children.** I won't join a company whose core business is advertising to kids. Not a negotiation, not a comp question. Adjacent sectors with kids as users I'd evaluate honestly — the core-business line is where I stop.
