# Job Applicant — Joelle Ashworth

Facts about my search. I'm a hospital clinical pharmacist moving into industry, so this file says plainly what I hold, what carries over to a Medical Science Liaison role, and what I haven't done yet.

## What I'm looking for

- Medical Science Liaison (MSL) in Medical Affairs at a pharmaceutical or biotech company. Field-based, individual contributor. MSL or Senior MSL; the title matters less than the pay floor below.
- Therapeutic area: cardiovascular. That covers anticoagulation and thrombosis, heart failure, lipids, and cardiometabolic or cardiorenal products.
- About 8 years since my PharmD in 2018: a PGY1 residency, then 7 years at Hollisbrook University Hospital in Philadelphia, the last 5 as the cardiology clinical pharmacist specialist.
- Location: home-based in Philadelphia, PA, covering a territory I can drive: Pennsylvania, New Jersey, Delaware, Maryland and the New York metro area. Not relocating.
- Travel: I expect the 50–70% field time most MSL postings list. I can do an average of 2 overnights a week.
- Compensation: current base $142K, no bonus. Target base $170K–$190K, floor $160K, plus an annual bonus (a 10–15% target is what I've seen) and a car allowance or fleet car. That's about $195K–$230K in total. Equity at a biotech is welcome, not required.
- Notice: 4 weeks, which is Hollisbrook's policy for clinical staff.

## Licensure and board certification

- Pennsylvania pharmacist license, active and unrestricted, since 2018.
- BCPS (Board Certified Pharmacotherapy Specialist) since 2020. BCCP (Board Certified Cardiology Pharmacist) since 2022.
- ASHP-accredited PGY1 residency, 2018–2019. ACLS and BLS current.
- I'll keep the license and both certifications active in industry, through continuing education.

## Where I have real depth

- Cardiology pharmacotherapy: anticoagulants and their reversal, antiplatelet therapy after stents, heart failure drug classes and titration, lipid-lowering therapy, antiarrhythmic dosing and monitoring. I round with cardiology teams every day.
- Evidence appraisal for real decisions: I led a contested formulary review for our pharmacy and therapeutics (P&T) committee and wrote the drug-use evaluation behind it (`Projects/Factor Xa Reversal Formulary Review.md`).
- Scientific communication: 72 teaching sessions for physicians and nurses since 2022, Department of Medicine grand rounds in 2025, an invited talk at a regional cardiovascular symposium in 2025, and senior author on one peer-reviewed brief report.
- Working relationships with physician thought leaders: I work daily with about 25 cardiologists, 3 of them my co-authors. Since 2024 I've been on the planning committee of the Northeast Cardiovascular Pharmacotherapy Symposium, where I've invited 9 speakers from 6 institutions.
- Turning evidence into a service: I built a pharmacist-led anticoagulation clinic with defined outcomes and kept it open through a budget fight (`Projects/Anticoagulation Transitions Clinic.md`).

- No CRM, territory plans, insight reports, or congress coverage for a company. My relationships are mostly within one health system and the symposium, not a mapped 5-state network.

## Search constraints — please respect these

- Medical Affairs only. No sales roles, and no "clinical educator" or "clinical specialist" roles that report to a commercial team or carry call targets. Physicians trust an MSL because the role is non-promotional, and I'm not giving that up.
- Cardiovascular therapeutic areas only, plus adjacent cardiorenal or cardiometabolic work. No oncology, CNS, vaccines, or rare disease outside cardiology. I'd be learning the science in front of the experts I'm supposed to serve.
- Postings that require prior MSL experience as a hard minimum are a poor fit; "industry experience preferred" is fine. Contract MSL roles through a contract organization only if they're W-2 with benefits and meet the base floor.
- No territory that needs weekly flights. I have a 6-year-old, and my partner works hospital shifts. I have a valid driver's license and a clean driving record, for a fleet car.
- No compounding, 503B outsourcing, or supplement companies. I want to represent products backed by randomized evidence.
