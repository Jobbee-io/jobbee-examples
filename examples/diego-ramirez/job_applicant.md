# Job Applicant — Diego Ramirez

I graduated in May 2026 (BS Computer Science) and I'm job hunting for my first full-time role. These are the facts about my search that don't fit in the settings fields.

## What I'm looking for

- **Junior Software Engineer / SWE I** roles. Any product stage is fine — I care more about who I'll learn from than the logo on the building.
- **Stack: open.** My coursework was C++/Java, my internships were TypeScript/React and Python, my capstone was Go. I don't have "my language" yet and I don't think I need one at this stage — pick me up on your stack and I'll get good at it.
- **Location: relocation-open, genuinely.** I'm in Austin, TX, and I have no ties keeping me here. Remote is also great. If you're somewhere with a real team I can learn from, I'll move.
- **Compensation:** I know junior bands vary a lot. I've been interviewing against offers in the $85K–$120K range and I don't have hard constraints. Tell me your band; I'll tell you honestly if it doesn't work for me.
- **Start date:** immediate.

## Work authorization — stated plainly

- I'm an F-1 international student (came to the US for university). I graduated in May 2026, so post-completion OPT runs 12 months and the STEM extension adds 24 — my work authorization is solid through **spring 2029**.
- **Realistically, I need H-1B sponsorship lined up within about two years** to stay ahead of the lottery cycle, and I'd rather say that in the first conversation than in week six. My last two employers didn't sponsor; both were still happy with the work. If your team has done this before, great — if not, I'm happy to share the standard process and cost ranges so nobody is guessing.
- After that: my long-term plan is the US. I'm not a visa-flight-risk story — I want to stay and build.

## What I actually bring

- **Internships where I shipped real code.** Two internships, both on teams with production systems. At Cartway I owned a payment-error state machine end-to-end (shipped, in production, handling real checkout failures) and I can walk through the bug I caused and fixed during week 4. Details in my project files.
- **Strong fundamentals.** Distributed systems (Raft capstone — I built a KV store that survives node kills and I know exactly where it's weak), databases, networks, OS. I like the unglamorous parts: correctness, tests, writing things down.
- **I finish things.** TA'd for 3 semesters, ran an autograder, maintained course infrastructure. The part-time grind of keeping 400 students' submissions flowing taught me more about on-call instincts than any class.

## Honest constraints — so nobody is surprised

- **I have never operated production at scale.** My production experience is internship-sized: real users, but not millions of them, and never with the pager. I'll be fast at learning it, but I'm not pretending I have it.
- **No experience being mentored *badly* and surviving** — both my teams were healthy. I don't know yet what my own bad habits look like under pressure; I'd like a team where someone tells me.
- My interview-testing stamina is a work in progress: strong on fundamentals and system reasoning, still leveling up on speed for leetcode-style rounds.

## What I want from my first team

- A designated mentor and real code review — I want my early PRs torn apart politely.
- Small enough that I see how features go from idea to deploy; big enough that someone has seen the failure modes before.
- On-call when I've earned it, not in month one.
- A team that writes things down. I will; it works best if it's a habit already.
