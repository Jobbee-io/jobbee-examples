## Where I have real depth

- Experiments as the unit of work. At Slatebird I've run 64 experiments to a readout written before launch: 17 shipped as wins, 32 flat, 9 losses, 6 invalid. The flat and losing ones are in the same log as the winners, and so is the one I announced as a win and later retracted (`Projects/Template Onboarding False Win.md`).
- Test design. Hypothesis in writing, one primary metric, guardrails, a sample size and a run time fixed before launch. Holdouts when the effect could fade. Intent-to-treat when people outside the test interfere with it.
- Lifecycle and onboarding. Behavior-triggered email and in-app programs, with activation defined so that it predicts paid conversion. My Dispatchwell onboarding program raised 14-day activation from 19.2% to 23.1%, and a 6-month holdout kept the gap.
- Paid acquisition with unit economics. Google Ads since 2018, plus Microsoft Advertising, LinkedIn and Meta. Offline conversion import, geo holdouts for brand search, CAC and payback defined with finance before anyone quotes them. At Slatebird I took non-brand paid CAC from $2,050 to $1,380 and payback from 24 to 16 months.
- Trial and pricing-page tests, with finance in the room. My trial-length test had 3 arms and no winner, and the follow-up it led to is the one that shipped.
- I write my own SQL (Snowflake) for readouts, and our analyst reviews it. I use Python for power calculations and simple pandas work. I'm not a data scientist. For anything beyond A/B tests and geo holdouts I work with one.

## Search constraints — please respect these

- Individual contributor only, no direct reports. I mentor an associate today and I'll keep mentoring. Managing people is a different job, and not the one I'm looking for.
- Product-led or product-led with sales assist, with a self-serve funnel (free trial or freemium) of about 8,000+ signups a month. At that volume a two-arm test on a 10% conversion rate can detect a 15% relative change in under 2 months. Below it, most revenue tests can't be read inside a quarter, and the job turns into guessing.
- Growth has to be able to change the product: onboarding, the trial, the pricing page. A paid-media-only seat or an MQL demand-gen role for a sales-led enterprise company is a different job.
- No sportsbooks, online casinos, payday lending or crypto trading apps. Growth work in those categories means getting people to do more of something that hurts some of them. I've said no to two recruiters from that space this year.
- Remote (US), or hybrid in Raleigh–Durham at 2 days a week or less. My partner's job and our house are here. Travel up to 10% is fine.
- Base floor $175K.

## How I work

- Hypothesis first, in one sentence: if we change X, metric Y moves by at least Z, because of W. If I can't write the "because", the test isn't ready.
- Definitions before numbers. Activation, CAC and payback each have a one-line definition, agreed with finance and data, before the first number goes out.
- No early calls. The run time is set before launch and I don't stop on a good-looking number.
- An activation win isn't a win until 60-day trial-to-paid agrees with it. That rule exists because of the template test.
- A losing test gets the same readout as a winning one: what we learned and what we'll try next.
