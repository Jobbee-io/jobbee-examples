# The magnetics should-cost model

Verdillo Power Systems, June – October 2023, one of my first projects as Senior Strategic Sourcing Lead. On my resume it's one bullet with 2 results, $648K and $2.05M a year. This file holds the model, the 3 negotiation sessions, what each number is measured against, and the pricing error the supplier caught in my model.

## The ask

In June 2023, at the annual renewal, Corrahan Magnetics asked for +9.5% on the 14 custom inductors and transformers it makes for our PCS. The letter cited copper, labor and energy. At our 2023 forecast volumes those parts cost $21.6M a year at the old prices, so the ask was worth $2.05M a year to them.

Corrahan is family-owned, with plants in Ohio and northern Mexico, and had been our only source for these parts for 6 years. Qualifying a second source would take about 9 months. They knew that.

## My role

I built the model, wrote the negotiation plan and led the sessions. A magnetics design engineer gave me winding specs, core sizes and wire gauges, and checked my numbers against the physics. Finance agreed the savings definitions with me before the first session. My director joined the last session and signed the agreement.

## The model

The top 5 parts were 78% of the spend, so I modeled those. For each part:

- Copper: wire weight times the copper price on the trailing 12-month average our contract specified, plus the wire maker's conversion cost.
- Core: material weight, priced from quotes I got from 2 core makers.
- Bobbin, insulation, varnish and terminals, from catalog prices.
- Labor: winding and test minutes, from the engineer's estimate and one plant visit, times a labor rate for the Mexico plant.
- 3% scrap, overhead, sales and admin cost, a 9% margin, and freight.

What it showed: the letter used the copper price from the spring 2022 peak. On the contract's own trailing average, the copper in these parts cost 7% less than at the last price reset. Copper is about 35% of the cost, so that's about −2.5% on price. The labor and energy increases were real, about +2.1%. Net, the justified change was about −0.4%. And at a 9% margin, the current prices were already 4% above my should-cost.

## The negotiation

1. Session 1, August. I shared the structure of the model, not my inputs, and asked Corrahan to put their numbers in it. They agreed on materials and labor and declined on overhead. Their engineer found my error in the first hour (below).
2. Session 2, 2 weeks later, with the corrected model. We agreed the copper number. They dropped the ask to +4%.
3. Session 3, September, with my director. We closed at −3.0% from October 1, 2023.

The agreement added a copper index clause: a quarterly adjustment on the copper share only, on a trailing 3-month average, in both directions. It runs 2 years and covers 80% of our forecast volume on the 14 parts, with ±20% quantity flex at 90 days' notice. Corrahan also got the 2 new magnetics parts for our 2024 PCS redesign.

What moved them from +4% to −3% was a trade, not the model alone. The copper clause took their biggest cost risk off the table. The 2 new parts were future business they wanted. And the model showed, part by part, that the old prices were already 4% above should-cost.

My walk-away, written down before session 2 and agreed with my director: above +1%, we start qualifying a second source.

## The numbers, with definitions

- Negotiated savings: (old price − new price) × forecast annual volume at signing. 3.0% × $21.6M = $648K a year. Measured against the last price paid; finance validated it.
- Cost avoidance: (quoted price − old price) × the same volume. 9.5% × $21.6M = $2.05M a year. Reported separately and never added to savings.
- Quote to final: $2.7M a year. I don't use this number. An opening ask is the supplier's to inflate.
- Realized savings, first 12 months (October 2023 – September 2024): $512K, the price difference times units actually received. It's lower than $648K because volume came in about 21% under forecast.
- The copper clause raised prices 1.8% for 2 quarters of 2024, about $150K. Finance tracks index changes separately in both directions, so they don't reduce or inflate the savings number.

## What went wrong

- My core prices were wrong for 2 of the 5 parts. I priced them as ferrite when they use a metal powder core, which understated their cost by about 11%. Corrahan's engineer found it in session 1. I re-issued the model 4 days later, but I spent the rest of that session defending the parts of the model that were right.
- I spent my first 3 weeks modeling all 14 parts before I checked where the spend was.
- I went into session 1 without a walk-away. My director asked what I would accept, and I didn't have an answer.

## What I'd do differently

- Walk the bill of materials with the supplier's engineer before the negotiation, not during it.
- Pareto the spend on day 1.
- Write the walk-away before the first session.

I've since reused the model's structure for film capacitors (2024) and busbars (2025). The same structure is the starting point for the 2027 magnetics renewal I'm running now.
