# Job Applicant — Megan Albright

Facts about my search that the settings fields can't hold. I work in compensation, so I've written this the way I'd want a pay recommendation written: the numbers, the structure, and the reason behind each limit.

## What I'm looking for

- Senior Total Rewards Partner or Senior Compensation Partner at a larger tech company. Individual contributor. I'm a Total Rewards Partner today at a 2,050-person healthcare software company. I want the next role to be bigger in scale and harder in design, not a team to manage.
- About 9 years in compensation, all of it hands-on: compensation analyst, senior compensation analyst, then compensation partner. Merit cycles, pay equity analysis, job leveling, sales compensation, and since 2024 medical plan cost and design.
- Location: Minneapolis is home. Remote (US) is my first choice. Hybrid in Minneapolis–St. Paul is fine. I won't relocate: my partner's job is here and our two kids are in school here.
- Compensation: current base $138K with a 10% target bonus, and no equity. Looking for $165K–$190K base, a 10–15% bonus target, and RSUs; total comp $200K–$260K depending on the equity. Base floor $160K. Please show me the band in the first call. I read bands for a living and I'll tell you straight away if it works.
- Notice: 3 weeks. One exception: if I accept an offer between mid-January and mid-March, I'll ask to start after April 1 so I don't walk out in the middle of a merit cycle I'm running. The 2027 cycle plan is written so someone else can run it, but I'd rather finish what I started.

## Where I have real depth

- Annual comp cycles. I've run 4 merit cycles at my current company (2023–2026). The 2025 cycle covered 1,912 employees with a 4.9% budget ($9.7M), finished at 4.82%, and needed 3 letter corrections.
- Pay equity analysis. I built the dataset and ran the regression for our first audit, under privilege with outside counsel. The adjusted gender gap in base pay went from 2.3% to 0.4% after a $574K remediation, and I rerun the model every cycle.
- Job architecture. I built the leveling framework that took 634 job titles down to 211 jobs across 3 tracks and 14 job families, with a salary range for every job.
- Sales compensation. At a manufacturer, I designed the move from commission on revenue to pay on gross margin for 120 quota carriers. Average discount off list fell from 19.4% to 15.8%.
- Benefits cost modeling. I modeled our 2026 medical plan change, got it wrong for our lowest-paid employees, and rebuilt it after they pushed back. It's the most useful project file I have.

## Where I'm thin

- Equity at a public company. I've built grant guidelines and cost models for our leadership incentive pool (about 90 people), but I've never run an annual RSU refresh cycle. That's part of why I want a larger tech company, and I'd rather say it than have it found.
- Non-US pay. Every employee I've supported has been in the US.

## Search constraints — please respect these

- IC roles only. No "Head of Total Rewards" and no compensation manager roles with direct reports. I mentor our analyst and I enjoy it. I don't want to manage a team; the work I'm best at is the design and the math.
- Compensation and benefits only. No HR business partner generalist roles, and no recruiting or talent acquisition roles of any kind. I'm not a recruiter. A role where most of the week is approving offers is a hiring job with a comp title, and I'll pass.
- Company size 3,000+ employees, with a total rewards team of at least 5. Our compensation work today is my director, one analyst and me. I want peers who will argue with my models.
- Industries: software, healthcare tech, fintech. No gambling or sports betting, no tobacco or vaping. Those are personal lines, not negotiable.
- Travel up to 10%. A few days on site for cycle planning is fine.

## How I work

- Aggregates only. I never put an individual's pay, rating or health information in a deck, a document or these files. Every number in my workspace is an aggregate, and I don't show any group smaller than 10 people. The pay equity numbers are the ones the company shared with all employees; the benefits numbers are plan-level totals.
- Every recommendation comes with a cost model and its assumptions written out. If a CFO can't trace a number back to its source, I haven't finished.
- I write for managers and employees, not for other comp people. If a manager can't explain the merit matrix in 2 sentences, I wrote it wrong.
- Tools: Excel (heavy, including Power Query), Workday Advanced Compensation, R for regression, basic SQL against our HR reporting database. I hold the CCP (Certified Compensation Professional), earned in 2021.
