# Promotion pay guideline

Orrinvale Health, written February to April 2024, in effect since May 1, 2024. One bullet on my resume. It came out of the pay equity audit (`Pay Equity Audit.md`): short work with a long tail, because every promotion since has gone through it. All figures are aggregates.

## Why

The audit traced most of its flagged cases to promotions. From 2020 to 2023, women's median promotion increase was 6.5% and men's 9.0%; the median for everyone was 7.6%. Managers set the amount with no guideline, and the gap lived in that discretion.

## Options

- A fixed percentage, for example 10%. Simple, but it overpays people already high in the new range and underpays big jumps.
- A guideline range, like the merit matrix. Managers would still pick inside the range, and that choice was where the gap came from.
- A floor plus a review: at least 8% or the new range minimum, whichever is more, and compensation reviews every promotion before approval. Chosen. The floor removes the low end, where the gap was; the review catches what a floor can't.

8% sat just above the old median. A 10% floor would have cost about $550K a year over past practice instead of $350K, and finance wouldn't fund that in the same quarter as the audit remediation. Two VPs argued a floor takes away a manager's judgment. My answer: judgment still decides everything above the floor. My director and the CHRO approved the guideline; the CFO approved the cost.

## Cost

Modeled on 2023 promotions: about $350K a year more than past practice, roughly 0.2% of base payroll. The merit cycle's promotion pot went from 0.8% in 2024 to 1.0% in 2025 (`Annual Merit Cycle.md`).

## The review

About 270 promotions a year: about 200 in the merit cycle and 70 off-cycle. Our analyst and I check 3 things: the increase meets the floor; the new pay sits sensibly in range against others in the same job; and the new level matches the manager's case against the level guide. Turnaround target: 2 business days. We don't decide who gets promoted, but we can send a case back when the guide doesn't support the level. In the 2025 cycle, 31% of promotions were raised during review, almost all to meet the floor or the new range minimum.

## The numbers, with their definitions

- Median promotion increase, women and men: 6.5% and 9.0% across 2020–2023, a 2.5-point gap. 10.0% and 10.2% in the 2025 merit cycle, a 0.2-point gap. 0.1 points in the 2026 cycle.
- The overall median rose to 10.0%, above the 9.0% I modeled. The new range minimum binds more often than I expected: from low in one range, reaching the next level's minimum often takes more than 8%. Off-cycle promotions showed it first, so I budgeted the 2025 cycle at a 10% median, and it landed there.

## What went wrong

The guideline went out on May 1 as a policy email, and managers didn't read it. From May to August 2024, about a third of off-cycle requests came in below the floor. I sent each one back, which cost those managers about a week and made the review feel like a gate. In September our HRIS partner added the floor to the Workday promotion form as a check, so a request under it can't be submitted. There have been none since. The check should have been there on May 1.

## What's still open

The guideline sets what a promotion pays, not who gets promoted. Promotion rates by gender and by race/ethnicity go into the cycle report I give the CHRO, in aggregate. Whether those rates are fair is a question for the talent team and leadership, and a closed pay gap on promotions doesn't answer it. I don't present it as if it did.
