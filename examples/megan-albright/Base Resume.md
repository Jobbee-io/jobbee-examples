## Experience

### Orrinvale Health — Total Rewards Partner (Apr 2024 – present); Compensation Partner (Aug 2022 – Mar 2024)

Revenue-cycle and patient-scheduling software for hospitals and clinics. Private, majority owned by a growth equity firm. About 1,800 employees when I joined, about 2,050 now, all in the US across 3 geographic pay zones. Total rewards team: my director, me, a benefits manager, and since 2024 a compensation analyst I mentor (she reports to my director). Retitled Total Rewards Partner when benefits moved into my scope.

- Annual merit cycles 2023–2026, 1,650 to 1,950 eligible employees a year. 2025: a 4.9% budget ($9.7M across merit, promotions and market adjustments), finished at 4.82%, 3 letter corrections out of 1,912 (19 in my first cycle in 2023). Full notes: `Projects/Annual Merit Cycle.md`.
- Job leveling framework (Oct 2022 – Jul 2023). 634 job titles held by 1,812 employees became 211 jobs across 3 tracks and 14 job families, each with a salary range. 157 employees raised to their new range minimum ($468K a year); 61 above maximum red-circled, with no pay cuts. Full notes: `Projects/Job Leveling Framework.md`.
- First pay equity audit (Sep 2023 – Apr 2024), run under privilege with outside counsel. I built the dataset and ran the regression in R; counsel's labor economist replicated it. Adjusted gender gap in base pay 2.3% → 0.4%; 168 employees adjusted, $574K a year (0.32% of base payroll). Full notes: `Projects/Pay Equity Audit.md`.
- Promotion pay guideline (2024), from the audit's root cause: every promotion gets at least 8% or the new range minimum, whichever is more, and compensation reviews each one before approval. The median promotion increase gap between women and men went from 2.5 points to 0.2. Full notes: `Projects/Promotion Pay Guideline.md`.
- 2026 medical plan change (May – Nov 2025). Modeled and proposed dropping the PPO plan, then reversed the key parts after employee pushback showed I'd modeled the average employee instead of the lowest-paid ones. Final design saves $0.98M against projected trend instead of $1.6M. Full notes: `Projects/Medical Plan Pushback.md`.
- Annual salary range refresh for all 211 jobs from 2 salary surveys; market pricing for about 60 new or changed jobs a year.
- Grant guidelines and cost models for the leadership incentive pool (about 90 participants). Supporting role on board compensation committee materials; my director presents.

### Brandvold Controls — Senior Compensation Analyst (Mar 2020 – Jul 2022); Compensation Analyst (Jun 2017 – Feb 2020)

Manufacturer of commercial HVAC and refrigeration controls, Bloomington, MN. About 4,300 employees: 2,700 hourly across 6 plants in Minnesota, Wisconsin and Iowa, and 1,600 salaried. Compensation team of 3: my manager (the compensation manager), another analyst and me.

- Sales compensation redesign (Apr – Dec 2020, live Jan 2021). Lead analyst and plan designer for 120 quota carriers, moving from commission on revenue to pay on gross margin dollars. Average discount off list 19.4% (2019) → 15.8% (2021); incentive cost as a share of gross margin dollars 7.9% → 7.6%. Full notes: `Projects/Sales Comp Plan Redesign.md`.
- Hourly wage structure rebuild (2021). 31 plant-specific pay rate tables became 5 pay grades with local market differentials, for 2,700 hourly employees. Cost $2.9M a year (2.3% of hourly payroll). I'll defend the structure. I won't claim the drop in hourly turnover that followed, because wages rose everywhere that year.
- Merit cycle support for 1,600 salaried employees, 2018–2022: the Excel manager worksheets, budget tracking and letters. 5 cycles, including the 2020 cycle that was deferred to October and cut to 2.0%.
- Market pricing for about 150 salaried benchmark jobs a year from 2 salary surveys; maintained the 12-grade salaried structure.
- Earned the CCP (Certified Compensation Professional) in 2021.

## Failures, indexed honestly

- 2025 merit cycle: I raised Engineering's skewed rating distribution at VP review, after managers had already built increases on it. It belonged before worksheets opened.
- Pay equity audit: my first model controlled for performance ratings, which would have hidden part of the gap. Counsel's economist caught it.
- Leveling: titles changed on the same day as the mapping letters, before managers had talked to their people. 11 engineers lost "Principal" from their title by letter.
- Medical plan: I modeled the average employee and announced a decision before asking anyone. Employees under $60K would have carried the cost.
- Sales comp: a margin definition reps couldn't see, a crediting bug that overpaid 14 reps, and a transition guarantee too short for reps with discount-heavy books.
