## The title note, first

A note on what I do versus what I'm called, because searches miss it otherwise: most of my actual work **is brand evangelism** — developer trust, technical narrative, community credibility, being the person who can stand in front of a hostile room of engineers and leave with them nodding. But almost nobody carries the title "evangelist" anymore, and the paid market prices this work under **Principal PMM / Staff PMM / Head of Developer Marketing**. So I keep the title precise and the description honest: if you're searching for a Principal PMM who does brand-evangelist work at a technical audience, that's me. If you're searching for a "Chief Evangelist" title, it probably pays less and does more keynotes; that's a different job.

## What I'm looking for

- **Principal PMM or Head of Developer Marketing**, on technical products: developer platforms, data infrastructure, dev tools, APIs. 14 years in: technical writer → product marketing → Senior PMM → Staff PMM → Principal PMM (big-tech marketing org today). I want a seat where marketing is a strategy function, not a content mill.
- **Stage:** growth-stage through public. I've launched at a scrappy pure-play (Halyard Tools) and run narrative inside a big-tech org (Verity Cloud); both taught me things the other couldn't. What I optimize for is org shape, not logo.
- **Scope I want, stated as outcomes:** I own the narrative and the launch architecture; I test messaging in real developer communities *before* it ships; I run win/loss as a strategic input, not a slide; and I have the standing to say "this product isn't ready for the story we want to tell" and be thanked for it.

## Where I have real depth

- **Positioning and narrative for technical products.** The kind that survives an engineer's red pen. My messaging test isn't a focus group — it's posting the draft where hostile experts live and reading what breaks. Full method in `Projects/Tier 1 Launch Api Platform.md` (including the test that flopped and what it saved).

Launches are systems — positioning, docs, demo, community, pricing story — and I build the whole machine.
- **Developer trust, rebuilt from negative.** I inherited a developer community that actively disliked my employer — sentiment panel at −34 — and ran an 18-month credibility program that took it to +12: one program worked, one backfired, both documented: `Projects/Developer Trust Turnaround.md`.
- **Win/loss as strategy.** Built the win/loss program at Halyard Tools from zero; it changed two roadmap decisions and killed one pricing model. That file is the proof PMM can be a strategic function: `Projects/Win Loss Program.md`.

Started as a technical writer (2 years, enterprise software — the PMM seat pulled me out of the docs in 2014) — I still write demo scripts and launch-page copy myself, and engineers can tell within one paragraph whether the person writing the narrative has read the docs or skimmed them.

## What I want — stated plainly

- **A seat at the product-strategy table as translator, not collateral.** PMM-as-translator means I'm in the roadmap debate representing the market; PMM-as-collateral means I'm handed the roadmap and asked to make it sound good. The interview question I ask every company: "tell me about the last time marketing changed the roadmap." Good orgs have an answer with a date on it.
- **CEO-accessible narrative ownership.** The company story should be consistent from the CEO's keynote to the docs landing page, and someone has to own that thread with authority. At Verity I own the developer-narrative slice of the corporate story; I want the whole thread at a smaller scale.
- **Orgs where PMM owns win/loss.** If win/loss lives in sales ops and produces a dashboard, the company is flying with instruments taped over. If it lives with PMM and produces roadmap decisions, we'll get along.

## What I won't take — also stated plainly

- **Demand-gen-only PMM roles.** If the PMM function's success metric is MQLs, the title is a costume and I'll be miserable by Q2.
- **"Marketing-ops with a PMM title."** Campaign tooling, webinar logistics, sales-slide formatting. Legit work, wrong job for me, and I've watched it hollow out good PMMs.
- **PMM reporting through sales enablement.** The reporting line tells you what the function is for. I've done it under product and under marketing; I won't do it under the sales-support org, because that's where narrative goes to be bullet-pointed.
- **Vanity-metric cultures.** If DevRel/marketing success is measured in followers and swag, the org is decorating. My numbers are activation, adoption, and pipeline — see the comp section for the bar I hold myself to.
