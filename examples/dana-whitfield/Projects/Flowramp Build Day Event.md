# Flowramp Build Day 2021: the first developer event I ran end to end

Halyard Tools, planned May to October 2021, held Thursday, October 14, 2021 in San Francisco and streamed live. Halyard had never run its own event; we sponsored other people's. I proposed it, owned the budget, and ran it from the first planning doc to the follow-up email. On my resume it's one line under Halyard Tools.

## Why an event, and what "worked" had to mean

By mid-2021 Flowramp had a year of GA behind it and a healthy self-serve funnel, but expansion inside our biggest accounts was slow. The engineers who used Flowramp liked it. Their platform leads, who approve wider rollouts, had never met us. My pitch to the CEO and the VP of Marketing: put the platform leads in a room with our engineers and our best customers, and let the customers do the selling.

I wrote the goal down before we booked a venue: 150 qualified conversations. A qualified conversation was one between a Halyard engineer or seller and someone from a target account, logged in the CRM the same day with one sentence on what they asked about. Not registrations and not livestream views. Registrations are easy to buy and tell you nothing.

## What I owned, and who did the rest

- **Me:** the goal and its measurement, the budget ($185K all-in), the agenda, speaker selection and coaching, the run of show, the follow-up plan.
- **An events contractor:** venue, catering, AV and the livestream vendor.
- **Two PMMs on my team:** speaker prep, and the content pack after the event.
- **Six Halyard engineers:** the build tables, plus a dry run the week before.
- **Sales:** invited their target accounts and staffed the conversation log.

## The agenda decisions

- **Customers on stage, not us.** Six of the eight talks were customers showing a real pipeline they ran in production, including what broke. Our only product talk was a 15-minute roadmap segment. I cut it from 30 minutes myself.
- **Build tables instead of a booth.** Four tables, each with two engineers and a laptop, where attendees brought their own pipeline problem. In the post-event survey it was the highest-rated part of the day.
- **Every talk had to end in something runnable.** Each speaker published an example repository the same day. That's what turned the talks into docs pages afterwards.

## The numbers

- **In person:** 310 registered, 226 attended, a 73% show rate. We over-invited because free events usually run around 50%.
- **Livestream:** 2,900 registered, 1,140 watched live, 640 at the concurrent peak.
- **Qualified conversations:** 212 against the goal of 150, from 61 target accounts.
- **Influenced pipeline:** $3.1M in the two quarters after, using the same definition as the launch work: sourced by, or touched within 90 days of, the program.
- **Cost per qualified conversation:** about $870. Our sponsored booths that year cost about $2,400 per logged conversation.
- **Content afterwards:** the 11 talks became 9 docs tutorials and 2 case studies within six weeks.

## What went wrong

- **The opening customer talk ran 22 minutes over.** I hadn't given speakers a hard stop or a timekeeper. Everything shifted, and the first build-table slot shrank by half. That was the slot the platform leads had come for.
- **Livestream audio dropped for nine minutes** during the second talk. The vendor's backup mic was on the same receiver as the main one. We posted a clean recording the next day, but live viewing never got back to the morning peak.
- **The follow-up email went out six days late.** The conversation log and the registration list had no shared account-name field, and matching them by hand took a week. The best-qualified people heard from us after the moment had passed. Sellers told me two expansions slipped a quarter because of it.

## What I'd do differently

- Hard stops, a visible timer and a timekeeper in the front row, agreed with every speaker at the dry run.
- Agree the data fields before the event: one account field, shared by registration and the conversation log. Write the follow-up email before the event and send it within 48 hours.
- Run it smaller, twice a year. The build tables carried the day, and they don't scale past about 250 people.
