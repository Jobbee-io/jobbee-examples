# Working with one creator: a six-month partnership for Ledgerline

Verity Cloud, February to July 2024. A paid, disclosed partnership with one independent data-engineering educator who runs a YouTube channel and newsletter called *The Quiet Join* (about 150K subscribers). It isn't on my resume at all. It's here because it shows how I picked one partner over a broad paid program, and what it takes to make paid work feel honest to developers.

## The full story

By early 2024 I had two lessons that pulled in opposite directions. In the 2023 Ledgerline launch, paid social hadn't reached data engineers; they listened to people they already learned from. And in the trust program, paying community members had backfired. So I proposed something narrow: pay one professional educator, label the work clearly, and leave the opinions to them.

The choosing was my work. I built a shortlist of 12 educators from what our design partners said they watched, read about 300 comments on each channel, and kept the three whose audience was mostly working data engineers. After three calls I picked *The Quiet Join*. I wrote one sentence of the terms myself, the line that we could correct facts but not opinions, and I set up the measurement so the numbers would compare with the launch. The rest was other people's work. The creator wrote, filmed and published every piece and kept editorial control. A named engineer answered the creator's questions, usually within a day. The docs team owned the pages the videos linked to, and I brought them in later than I should have.

Four videos and two newsletter issues went out between February and July. Two things went wrong. Video 2 was recorded on a preview build, a default changed at GA, and viewers following along hit an error. We pinned a correction within 48 hours, and the creator recorded a short update without extra pay. Video 3 called our pricing page confusing. It was. The page was rewritten within three weeks, and that video did best of the four.

The paid work ended in July, as the contract said. It brought about 1,140 trial workspaces at about $42 each, against about $310 for the 2023 paid social, and $1.9M of influenced pipeline over the next two quarters. Two of the 14 feature requests from the comments shipped in the next release, and the creator covered both unpaid.

What's still open: I've run this once, with one creator, on a product developers already trusted. I don't yet know if it works with a second creator, or for a product with a weaker story.

## Why one creator

Two earlier lessons pulled in opposite directions. Paid social had failed for the 2023 Ledgerline launch: data engineers ignored ads but listened to people they already learned from (`Projects/Ledgerline Launch Campaign.md`). And paying community members had backfired in the trust program (`Projects/Developer Trust Turnaround.md`). My rule from both: don't pay enthusiasts to sound like enthusiasts. Pay a professional educator for clearly labeled sponsored work, and leave the opinions to them. Trust was the asset, and it doesn't split across ten partners.

## Choosing

- A shortlist of 12 educators, built from what our design partners said they watched.
- For each channel I read about 300 comments. The bar: more than 60% self-identified working data engineers. Three channels cleared it.
- The deciding test: had they criticized a vendor fairly on camera, and was every past sponsorship disclosed? *The Quiet Join* had a well-known video taking apart a competitor's benchmark, with sources, and labeled every sponsorship in the first ten seconds.
- Three calls, then one choice. The creator asked more about our limits than our features.

## Terms

- A flat fee of $48K for four videos and two newsletter issues over six months. No performance bonus: a bonus tied to sign-ups changes what someone says.
- Disclosure in every video and description.
- The creator kept editorial control, including the right to say what Ledgerline doesn't do. We could correct facts before publishing, not opinions. I wrote that sentence into the contract myself.
- Verity provided early access to the 2024 features, a test environment with realistic data volumes, and a named engineer who answered the creator's questions within a day.

## Measurement and results

Same definitions as the launch, so the numbers compare. Each video and issue had its own tracked link, plus "how did you hear about us" on the trial form. Influenced pipeline meant sourced by, or touched within 90 days of, the partnership.

- 4 videos, about 410K views in total; the two newsletter issues reached about 60K subscribers each.
- 9,800 docs visits and 1,140 trial workspaces from the tracked links: about $42 per trial workspace, against about $310 for the 2023 paid social.
- 38 design-partner applications and $1.9M influenced pipeline over the next two quarters.
- The video comments produced a ranked list of 14 feature requests. Two shipped in the next release, and the creator covered both, unpaid.

## What went wrong

- **Video 2 used a preview build.** One default changed at GA, and viewers following along hit an error: about 60 comments in two days. We pinned a correction within 48 hours, and the creator recorded a three-minute update without extra pay. I now share the release calendar with any partner and pin every demo to a released version.
- **Video 3 called our pricing page confusing.** It was. We didn't ask for a cut; we rewrote the page within three weeks and said so in the comments. It became the best-performing video of the four.

## What I'd do differently

Start with three months and an option to extend. Put the correction process in the contract from day one. Bring the docs team in before the first video, so the pages the videos link to are ready.
