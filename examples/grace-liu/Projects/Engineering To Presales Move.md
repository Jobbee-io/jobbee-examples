# From backend engineer to solutions engineer

Cairnwatch, from mid-2021, when I was asked, through 2022, my first full year in presales. On my resume it's one bullet under the SE role. Every presales interview asks why I left engineering and whether I'd go back.

## How it started

At Saltpine Health in 2018 I ran our observability vendor evaluation from the buyer side. The Cairnwatch SE rebuilt 2 of our dashboards overnight so we could compare like with like. I joined Cairnwatch in May 2019 on the ingest pipeline. From 2020 I took the escalation rotation, 1 week in 6, and SEs pulled me into 14 prospect calls between early 2020 and July 2021 when ingest questions got deep. In June 2021 Renata, the Enterprise West SE manager, asked if I'd move.

## The decision

I wrote a 3-page decision doc before I answered.

- Pay: my engineering base was $165K, with RSUs worth about $35K a year and no cash bonus. The SE offer was $210K OTE: $147K base and $63K variable (70/30), paid quarterly on the Enterprise West new-ARR quota and capped at 150%, with RSUs of about $25K a year. My base would drop $18K, and whether I came out ahead depended on a region I didn't control.
- Measurement: from shipped code and incident counts to technical wins and a region's number.
- Daily life: about 25% travel, a calendar set by prospects, 8 or more live deals at once.
- The gaps: discovery, executive demos, objections, working inside an AE's deal strategy, and saying "I don't know, I'll find out" to a customer.

I asked for 3 terms and got all 3: a 2-quarter variable guarantee at 100%, a written 6-month return path to the ingest team at my senior level, and a named mentor with a 90-day ramp plan. I didn't use the return path.

## The ramp

The plan was to shadow 20 calls, run 10 discovery calls with review, pass demo certification and co-run one POC in 90 days. I shadowed 23 and ran 11. I failed my first demo certification: 41 minutes of features in a 25-minute slot. I passed the retake 2 weeks later in 18 minutes, built around the one problem the scenario buyer had. My first solo technical win came in March 2022, month 6.

## The numbers

- 2022: 12 wins on 24 decided deals (50%), against 44% for the region, on the definitions in my presales record.
- 2022 cash: $147K base plus $58.7K variable (Q1 paid by the guarantee, the other 3 quarters at the region's payout) came to $205.7K, against a $165K base in 2021. RSU value fell by about $10K a year.

## What went wrong in year one

- My first executive demo was 35 minutes of features for a CIO who wanted 5 minutes on cost. The AE had to rescue the meeting.
- I told an engineer "yes, we can do that" about per-index retention. It needed a professional-services engagement and the AE had to walk it back. Now I say I'll confirm in writing by a named day, and I do.
- I went deep with a prospect's engineer while my AE was trying to get a meeting with that engineer's VP. I was winning the technical conversation and slowing the deal.
- Nobody had defined "technical win", one of the numbers SEs were judged on. I found that out in month 4. With Renata I wrote the definition: a written note from the prospect that their success criteria were met. The region adopted it in 2022.

## What I'd change, and would I go back

I'd get the measurement definitions in writing before moving, not only the comp plan. I'd learn discovery before demos. And I'd keep a weekly coding habit from day one; I came back to it in 2023 with the demo environment (`Demo Environment Rebuild.md`), and my POCs got better.

Would I go back? Not to a backlog. I still write code every month for POCs and demos, and that engineering base is why prospects' engineers trust my answers.
