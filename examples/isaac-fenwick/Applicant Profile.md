# Job Applicant — Isaac Fenwick

Facts about my search that the settings can't hold. I'm a product designer, so these files also do a job my portfolio can't: a resume gets read before anyone opens a portfolio link, and the resume is cut from what's written here.

## What I'm looking for

- Staff or Principal Product Designer, individual contributor. I've been a Senior Product Designer at Rosterwell since April 2023. Rosterwell makes booking, scheduling and payments software for fitness and wellness studios. Since 2024 my work has crossed team lines: the enterprise admin console, a weekly usability testing practice other designers now run, and leading design across two teams since July 2025, still as a Senior IC with no reports.
- About 8 years of design work, since June 2018: almost 2 years in graphic design at a brand and packaging studio, almost 2 as a UX designer at a digital product agency, and almost 5 in product design at Rosterwell.
- Products I know well: two-sided products with an operator side and a consumer side, self-serve B2B trials and onboarding, admin tools for customers with many locations, and mobile booking flows.
- Location: Los Angeles. Hybrid in LA up to 3 days a week, or remote in the US. I'm not relocating. Travel for customer visits is welcome, up to about 6 trips a year.
- Compensation: current base $182K, a 10% target bonus, and private-company RSUs worth about $40K a year at the last tender-offer price. Looking for $210K–$245K base and $290K–$370K total comp. My base floor is $205K.
- Notice: currently employed. Two weeks' notice plus one week off, so I could start about three weeks after an offer.
- Why now: in April 2026 Rosterwell reorganized design under a new VP. The level above Senior is now Design Lead, which has 3 to 5 direct reports. I was offered that role in June 2026 and said no. There is no IC level above Senior there anymore.

## Where I have real depth

- Onboarding and activation for self-serve B2B trials. I wrote the activation definition with a PM and an analyst before I drew anything, then moved it from 31.4% to 39.8% in a 6-week A/B test.
- Admin tools for customers with many locations. The enterprise console redesign cut median time on 5 benchmark tasks by 69% to 87% and how-do-I tickets per 100 locations by 61%.
- Research as part of the design job. I co-founded a weekly usability slot at Rosterwell and have moderated about 90 sessions since January 2024. I'm not a researcher: the protocol, consent and moderator training belong to our researcher, and I say so.
- Booking flows on both sides of the counter: a mobile service-booking flow for 72 auto shops at the agency, and class booking in Rosterwell's member app, including the redesign I had to roll back.
- Visual craft, with a bias I know about. I started in graphic design and I'm still the person on the team who fixes the type scale. I also learned, at a cost, that for people who use a screen every day, density beats polish.

## Search constraints — please respect these

- Individual contributor only. No design manager roles and no "Design Lead" roles with direct reports; I just turned one down. No "Head of Design" as the first designer at a small startup either: that's hiring and managing plus all of the design.
- The Staff scope has to be real. A design team of at least about 8 designers and at least one researcher, so there are cross-team problems to own. A senior role with a new title isn't a fit.
- I need access to users and to product data. If designers can't talk to customers or see the funnel, I can't do the work I'm describing here. At the agency I shipped a booking flow I could measure for 8 weeks and never again.
- No sports betting, casino-style games, or payday and high-interest consumer lending. I design booking and checkout flows, I know which levers make people commit faster, and I won't pull them for a product whose margin is the user's loss.
- No agencies or consultancies. I want to own outcomes for years, not until launch day.
- Design exercises: paid, or time-boxed to 4 hours. I'll happily walk a panel through one of my projects in depth instead; it shows more than a weekend of spec work.

## How I work

- I define the success metric with the PM and the analyst before the first sketch, and the definition goes into the brief.
- I test with the people who carry the metric. If regulars make 70% of bookings, regulars are in the sessions.
- Low fidelity first. I test while the decision is still open, not to collect a stamp on a finished design.
- Decision docs for anything big: what we chose, what we rejected, and why.
- I keep project notes current as work ends. The files in `Projects/` are my case studies in words.

**Q:** Your profile says your portfolio is password-protected because of an NDA, but your resume and project files give activation rates, task times and ticket changes from Rosterwell. Which of these numbers can appear on a resume?
**A:** Fair question, and the two facts did pull against each other. In March 2025 I asked Rosterwell's comms lead in writing what designers may use in portfolios and resumes. Cleared: rates, percentages and percentage-point changes, task times, test sample sizes, and the public figure of about 8,500 studios. Not cleared: revenue, customer names, and absolute volumes such as trials, bookings or ticket counts per month. So "activation 31.4% to 39.8%" and "how-do-I tickets per 100 locations down 61%" can go on a resume; raw counts can't. Some raw counts stay in my project files because they're my working notes, but they never go on a resume. The agency work is different: Corbel Auto Care approved the case study in 2021, including its name and the rates in `Projects/Mobile Service Booking.md`.
