# Thursday usability sessions: a weekly testing slot any team can use

Rosterwell, January 2024 to now. On my resume this is a single bullet under Rosterwell, and it isn't in my portfolio at all. It's still the project I'd most want a Staff interview to ask about, because it's a practice that now runs without me. I co-own it with Tessa, our UX researcher, and this file says which parts are hers.

## Why it was needed

In 2023 Rosterwell had 12 product designers and one researcher. Research worked as a queue. The median wait from a request to a first session was 24 days, so most teams didn't ask. Going back through 2023's release notes, I could find usability testing before build for 9 of the 41 features that had design work: 22%. Then my class schedule redesign lost in an A/B test (`Class Schedule Rollback.md`), partly because the little testing I did was with the wrong people. That was the push.

## How it works

- Every Thursday, three 40-minute moderated remote sessions.
- A team claims a slot by Monday noon with a prototype, three tasks, and the one open decision the session will change. No open decision, no slot.
- An opt-in panel, recruited through invitations in the member app and the studio dashboard, in five segments: studio owners, front-desk staff, enterprise admins, new members, and regular members. Each slot is recruited from the segment that carries the requesting team's metric.
- Incentives: a $60 gift card for members, $100 for studio owners and staff. Enterprise admins get no incentive under their contracts; they get a short summary of what changed because of their session.
- Moderators: Tessa, me, and 6 designers Tessa trained, each with two shadowed sessions and two supervised ones.
- Anyone can observe. A one-page "what we saw" note goes out by Friday noon. Every finding goes into a tracker with an owner and a decision: fix, won't fix, or needs more evidence. PMs review the tracker for 30 minutes once a month.

## My part, and Tessa's

I proposed it and ran the logistics for the first six months, about 20% of my time next to my design work: the sign-up board, the note template, the panel invitation copy, and the reminder flow. I've moderated about 90 sessions through October 2026. Tessa owns the protocol, consent and data handling, and moderator training; she decides what we must not ask participants and how recordings are kept. Since July 2024 a rotating designer runs the logistics each quarter. Since June 2025 our second researcher manages the panel.

## The numbers, through October 2026

- 128 Thursdays held out of 147 possible. We skipped holidays, two company offsites, and a few weeks with no requests.
- 343 completed sessions out of 384 booked. No-shows: 24% in the first six weeks, about 11% overall, 7% in 2026.
- Features tested with users before build: 22% (9 of 41) in 2023, 81% (47 of 58) in 2025.
- Median wait from request to first session: 24 days → 6 days.
- 61 different people observed at least one session in 2025, including 17 engineers.
- 406 findings logged; 64% fixed or explicitly declined within a quarter.
- Cost: about $11K a year in incentives, plus everyone's time.

What I can't prove is that this made the product better. One proxy: launches rolled back or redesigned within a quarter went from 5 in 2023 to 1 in 2025. The team also got more experienced over those two years, so I treat that as a correlation and say so.

## What went wrong

- I pitched it to the whole design team in November 2023 before I'd talked to Tessa. She heard about a research program secondhand, in her own field. I apologized, we rewrote the proposal together, and she's been co-owner since. It's the mistake I'm least proud of in this file.
- No-shows ran at 24% for six weeks because reminders went by email only. We added text reminders the day before and an hour before, plus one standby participant every Thursday.
- Repeat participants. By month 5, 18% of sessions were with people on their third session or more, and they had started reviewing our designs like experts. We capped it at one session per person per quarter and refreshed the panel.
- Validation theater. In the first months about a third of the slots brought finished, high-fidelity designs a week before build, mostly to say the work had been tested. The "one open decision" rule came in April 2024 to stop that.
- Enterprise admins were too hard to recruit every week. They moved to one dedicated Thursday a month.

## What I'd do differently

- Bring the researcher in on day one, as the owner of the method, before any pitch.
- Set the participant cap from the start.
- Measure the baseline before launch. I rebuilt the 2023 number from release notes, so it's approximate.
